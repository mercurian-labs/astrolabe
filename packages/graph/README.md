# @mercurian/graph

The Atlas Graph proof of concept: a memory (a vault of atomic notes linked by wikilinks) drawn as a web in three dimensions, showing a thread's reading. Notes surface as the agent's turns reach them. In T3 Code it is the Graph surface beside a thread (`apps/web/src/components/GraphPanel.tsx`), which tells the thread's checkpoints over the files they changed: a turn strip above the map steps, picks, and plays the turns (Live returns to the working turn and to following), the turn's moves ride beside the agent's cursor in its popover, and a picked turn reveals its request in the conversation.

## Run the playground

```sh
vp dev        # from packages/graph; serves playground/ on Vite's default port (5173)
vp build      # builds the playground into dist-playground/
vp test run   # model, layout, and scheduler tests
```

## What to try

The rule: still at rest, organic in motion. A running turn alone moves nothing; only a note whose place changes moves, drifting a little on its way and landing exactly. Hovering, picking, and selecting a play change ink, size, labels, and edges, never a note's place, and a held hover settles to no frames within about half a second.

- **Step the thread.** Press Play, or step the checkpoints. Every note has a home in the force layout that never changes; the thread's reading gathers its notes into a cluster in front, grown from the previous checkpoint's, so notes already gathered barely move while new arrivals travel in from home. Notes leaving the reading spring home. While a turn runs (Play, or 1.8 s after a manual step; the debug readout says "turn running") its story stays lit; nothing moves for the turn itself. Amended notes carry an accent dot; PR Review fills its red link in place (turn 6); the turn's new links draw in full accent and ring with a damped wave, the removed link draws broken (turn 7); red links reached toward appear hollow (turns 8–9). "this turn only" narrows to the current turn.
- **Follow the play-by-play.** The column on the right lists the checkpoint's moves in order: notes read, amended, and written, links made, removed, and reached toward a red link. Play walks the plays for you: each checkpoint's plays in turn (1.6 s each, 2.4 s for a link, so its beads run), then a one-second beat with the whole turn lit, then the next checkpoint; Pause holds the play on screen, and Play resumes after it. Click a play (or focus the list and use the arrow keys) to make it the story: its notes come to the front with outlined labels, its link alone reads in full (with beads if it was made), and the rest of the turn's links stay dark. A playback step or a play selection lets go of any hover, so the story is never dressed as one. It holds until you click it again, pick another, press Escape, or change checkpoint.
- **Watch the edges.** At rest no edge is drawn, in any reading, so no label has a line under it; worked notes glow but do not light their links. Hovering or picking a note lights its links as hairlines (about 1.5 px), and only a real pointer does that: a note that glides under a still pointer does not hover; a search keeps only links between matches; a shown lens holds its edges at least at 35%. The story lights transiently, while the turn runs and through the landing window (4 s), then falls to nothing: the turn's new and broken links in full (about 2 px), with accent beads running each new link the way it was written (both ways for a link added both ways). It never lights a note's neighbourhood: the links an arriving or worked note has stay dark. The story's notes read in full ink at any depth, their labels outlined in paper and drawn over every line, as a hovered or picked note's label is. With "this turn only", only the turn's own links light. The debug readout's "beads" line counts the running beads; it returns to 0, and frames/s to 0, when the story falls.
- **Take the camera.** The graph lands at the reading density (the spacing between the reading's settled notes spans about 96 pixels) and never zooms out past it to fit anything: each checkpoint step, while following, centres on the turn's story (what it wrote or amended and the ends of the links it added, or their densest part when they spread wider than the frame), and the rest of the reading lies beyond the frame, reached by panning. Notes are small dots the same size in every memory (a lone note about 3.7 px, hubs at most 1.6 times that); the debug readout's "radius px" and "edge px" lines give the on-screen sizes at the camera's target. Drag to turn and scroll to zoom toward the pointer, from a quarter to four times that density. Following stops and the chip at the bottom says so; Resume frames the reading again. Fit returns to the reading density on the pick, else on the reading's centre. Pick a note: it grows and its links light and ring once; nothing is rearranged, and a note the reading does not hold is picked where it stands.
- **Read the map.** Linger on a note to see its popover. Turn on the Product and Decisions lenses and hover a tinted edge to see its type. Hide red links, hide orphans. Switch to Flat to compare the same web with z pinned.
- **Feel the scale.** The memory menu offers synthetic memories of 600, 3k, 10k, and 100k notes (100k takes a few seconds to load), each with a synthetic thread of 10, 50, or 200 checkpoints: turns read within a cluster and now and then far away (the gathering travels), amend, fill red links, link across clusters, and sometimes unlink. Up to 3,000 notes the home layout is settled by forces; past that, notes are placed (groups on a lattice, hubs at their group's centre), which takes about 0.06 s at 10k and 0.5 s at 100k where forces would take about 30 s at 10k. The readout's "layout" line gives the time. The graph draws only what the reading names (its notes, their neighbours within depth, and what a hover, pick, or search adds), from slots sized to that (the readout's "slots"), so a step, a frame, and a hover cost the reading, never the memory; "whole memory" shows every note and costs it. Only what is in view is labelled, and only lit edges are drawn at all. The flat renderers give labels a budget by the frame's size (one per 96 × 48 pixels, at most 400) and keep only labels that overlap none kept before them, the story's first, so a narrow panel names its worked notes whole and the rest give way. A label that would cross the frame's right edge reads on its note's left, or is not drawn: none is clipped. The turn's own notes' labels (and a hovered or picked note's) are never cut: one that would collide with another flips side, then stacks a line above or below.
- **Watch the agent write.** The dev server keeps the almagest notes as files in `.workspace/` (copied from `$ATLAS_VAULT`, default `~/dev/mercurian/almagest`, fresh on every start; the vault is only read). The thread's amends, writes, links, and unlinks are written to those files, and every change to a file there, from the thread or from you in a shell, opens a read-only Atlas Editor on that note with the change highlighted and the agent's cursor after it. A selected play writes that play alone (rewinding the file first); a checkpoint with no play selected writes the rest of its turn. Try `perl -pi -e 's/memory/MEMORY/' .workspace/Memory.md`. Moving the thread resets the files it writes, so a shell edit to one of those lasts until the next step. Deletions change the text but are not highlighted.
- **Read a codebase.** The code stories (the memory menu's `code ·` entries) are codebases: every symbol sits in its file, every file in its folder (`parent`), and links say what they are (`kind`: calls, imports, inherits…, and `resolution`: exact, overload, or heuristic). A container whose notes show is a region, not a node: a faint disc around them with its name small at the edge, nested folder › file › class, alternating tint by depth (the tint and the one-pixel rim are the same lightness step from the paper in a dark appearance as in the light one); it has no dot, no label in the pool, and nothing to pick. A container with nothing shown inside is an ordinary note. The homes nest the same way (each container's children settle inside its disc, roots by the links between their contents; packed past 3,000 notes), and the gathering keeps a file's symbols together, sibling discs apart, at about the homes' spacing. With `quietAncestors` (a host's choice; the T3 Code Graph surface sets it, the playground does not), only the story's group (the lowest region holding the turn's story) and its parent draw; the regions above them draw bare, no disc and no name, and the parent carries their path ("apps/web/src/components"), so the frame holds the work and one named level of context rather than rims of every ancestor. With `fillFrame` (the Graph surface sets it), the folders holding the story open while the frame can hold what shows, the one holding most of the story first, so a story spanning the repository's top reads as its folders and a wider frame shows at least what a narrower one did; files stay the default level. A closed container says what it holds after its name ("web · 190 files"). A codebase's reading starts at depth 0: the worked notes and the ends of the turns' links, nothing receded around them (raise it in Settings). A guessed (heuristic) link draws dashed when lit, and inherits, implements, and overrides draw with arrowheads; with the Relationships lens on, hovering an edge shows its kind and resolution. The play-by-play becomes a tree: one row per file with its moves counted ("reading.ts · wrote 3, amended 2, linked 5"), collapsed; open it to see each symbol's moves and, under each symbol, its links ("→ playEdge (calls)"). A file row selects all its plays together, a symbol row its moves, a link row that link; the file holding a selected play opens by itself, and Play still walks the plays one at a time in turn order.
- **Read a codebase at its level.** A container is open (a region) or closed (one node, sized by the notes it holds, named like a note). Each checkpoint lands at the level of its story: the lowest container holding everything the turn wrote, amended, or linked opens, its children show one level below it (each child container closed), and everything else in the reading closes where its line meets that one, so earlier work persists, collapsed. When that would not fit one frame at the reading density, the level goes up a container at a time; Fit does the same for the whole reading. Links aggregate to what shows: one line per pair of shown things, standing for every link between their contents (dashed only when all of them are guesses); a link inside one closed container draws nothing, and a link into an open container ends at the member that shows. Click a closed container to open it (the camera frames it); press Escape, or click an open region's name, to close it again. Choosing a symbol's or a link's play opens whatever closed container holds it; a file's plays stay at the level, their beads running on the aggregated links. Linger on a note whose lit links end off the frame and the camera pans just enough to bring them in, and returns when the hover ends.
- **Watch the agent work.** When a checkpoint lands, the agent's cursor (the editor's agent cursor's twin, in the story colour, its "Agent" label showing while it rests) visits the turn's moves in the play-by-play's order, resting on each, its label naming each move; for a link it travels the link from source to target. The play-by-play row lights in step, and choosing one play puts the cursor on that move. A host can open the turn's moves in a popover beside the cursor instead of its chip (`popover`; clicking the chip asks for it): the rows the cursor is telling are lit, the rest is scrollback, and choosing a row chooses that play. When the tour ends, what it opened is closed (only that: a container the reader opened meanwhile stays open) and the camera fits the survey to the frame at the reading density. A resize keeps the tour and what it opened (the level re-fits to the new frame, and is framed again, once the tour is in), and a graph mounted again on a checkpoint it already landed (a tab switch, a pane that moved) frames it without landing it again. A turn that grows while the agent works (the same checkpoint, more moves) does not land again: the cursor goes on to tell the tour of the new moves alone (in their order, with their own verbs) after the ones it is telling, and rests once done. The flat renderers keep every settled note where it stands while the universe grows; a note new to the view is placed once at its group's edge, and the next landing regathers. Each move tells what happened, once, then stills: a written note draws itself (a ring closing into a dot, one ripple), an amended one is swept around once and its fill refreshes, a read one is glanced at (a halo), and a removed one (a code story's removed symbol losing its last link) drains to an outline that fades. The story no longer grows notes: size is connectedness; only a hover or a pick grows one. The checkpoint's written, amended, and removed notes carry "+", "~", and "×" before their names, on the map and in the play-by-play, and its worked notes keep a hairline ring in the story colour until you step on. 3D and Flat tell it the same way, the cursor and its chip drawn in screen space.
- **Compare renderers.** "3D | Flat | Lean | Pixi" picks how the graph is drawn (remembered across reloads). 3D and Flat are one renderer with two cameras and glide into each other. Lean draws the flat reading on a stripped-down Three.js renderer (no depth machinery; labels are glyph quads drawn straight from the text atlas, one draw, so a pan or zoom costs them nothing) and Pixi draws it on Pixi.js; both follow Flat's rules, and switching to or between them keeps the selection, the play, and the flat view. The debug readout shows the mode, the last frame's draw calls, the time in the render call itself and the preparation around it (mean / max over the last 60 frames that rendered). Bench resets to a reference workload (the synthetic 600-note memory, whole, with no search, filter, lens, pick, play, or playback), fits, waits for rest, glides every note for three seconds, and prints both times for the current mode, so pressing it in each mode compares them; it restores nothing afterwards.

## Run the bench

The bench measures every renderer on identical scripted steps: `load`, `rest` (ten seconds that must cost no frames and no timers, counted on a graph mounted after the timers, intervals, and animation frames are wrapped), `glide`, `story` (the synthetic thread's first checkpoints landing, gathering, and running beads), `pan`, `zoom`, and `hover`. Each run starts from the same reference workload (the synthetic thread's reading at checkpoint 5, what the graph draws by default: nothing searched, filtered, lensed, picked, selected, or hovered, following, fitted, its landing story fallen, at rest), whatever ran before; a warm-up run is discarded, and the medians of three runs are reported: render ms around the render call alone (GPU timer queries sit outside it, in prep), prep ms for the rest of the frame, presented-frame delta, draw calls, labels drawn and cut, overlapping labels, edges, beads, the share of presented link-hold frames whose beads moved, long animation frames overlapping the step (taken after the last frame's entries land), GPU ms where the browser has a timer query (none across a disjoint event), and the JS heap.

Open `/bench.html` on the dev server to pick a matrix, run it, and copy or download the JSON. Or, with the dev server running:

```sh
pnpm bench                           # every mode × 600 / 3k / 10k × every scenario, compared with bench/baseline.json
pnpm bench:quick                     # every mode at 600 notes, glide, pan, rest, one run
node bench/run.ts --sizes 100000 --runs 1 --scenarios load,rest,glide
node bench/run.ts --write-baseline   # save this run as the baseline
```

The runner opens headed Chromium with the GPU, writes `bench/results/<date>.json` and `.md` (not checked in), and exits 1 when a cell's p95 render time rose more than 15% over the baseline. `bench/baseline.json` is a starting point from one machine (it records which), not a standard.

## Regenerate the fixture

```sh
node scripts/index-vault.ts <vault-dir> [out-file]
```

The script reads the vault's top-level notes and skill maps and writes `fixtures/almagest.json`. It never writes into the vault.

## Shape

- `src/model`: pure data: indexing, the thread's reading per checkpoint, the view (what is shown and how prominently), lenses, search, and settings.
- `src/layout`: the home layout (forces up to 3,000 notes, placed past that; computed once per memory and forces), the gathering, and critically damped springs.
- `src/render`: the renderers behind one interface (`renderer.ts`) and what they share (targets, the story and its beads, the node state's springs, the frame scheduler that sleeps when nothing moves). `AtlasRenderer` is Three.js directly for 3D and Flat: one instanced billboard mesh for nodes, one instanced ribbon mesh for edges, both reading node positions from a float texture, troika SDF labels, CPU picking. `flat/FlatRenderer` is the 2D controller that Lean (`lean/`, Three.js) and Pixi (`pixi/`, Pixi.js) draw for. `AtlasGraph` takes the renderer as a factory, so a host loads only the one it draws with: the package entry exports `createLeanRenderer` alone, and the playground picks from `render/renderers.ts`.
- `src/AtlasGraph.tsx`: the thin React component around the renderer.
- `src/FileGraph.tsx` (entry `@mercurian/graph/file`): a file's local graph for Repositories, without a thread: rings around the file laid out in one pass (`model/fileGraph.ts`), drawn by Lean with its links at rest, the one place a link draws without attention.
- `fixtures`: the almagest vault index, its scripted thread, and the synthetic memories and threads.
- `bench`: the bench's scenarios, sampling, report, harness (run by `playground/bench.tsx`), and the Playwright runner.
