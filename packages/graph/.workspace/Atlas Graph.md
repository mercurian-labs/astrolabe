# Atlas Graph

The [[Atlas]]'s picture of the whole [[Memory|memory]] as a graph in three dimensions: every note a node, every wikilink an edge, and the frontier drawn as the red-linked nodes it is. It is what Obsidian users know as the graph view, built over the memory's own index rather than any editor's. It is a [[Thread Tabs|thread tab]]: it opens beside a thread, draws the memory as that thread's branch has it, and shows the **thread's reading**, where nodes surface as the agent's turns reach them. _(2026-09-26: it first had a second reading, the whole memory on the team's Memory page, dropped as recorded below.)_ Where a [[Skill Maps|skill map]] shows a curated subgraph for a purpose, the Atlas Graph shows every note, with nothing selected and one node per note. _(2026-09-30: the same surface drawn over a codebase, symbols for notes and calls, extensions, and implementations for links, is the [[Code Graph]].)_ _(Designed 2026-09-24. The three dimensions and the surfacing come from Matt Pocock's AI Coding Dictionary, whose three-dimensional graph turns and brings terms into view as you explore them.)_ _(2026-10-23: drawn in two dimensions only, as resolved under Open Decisions.)_

## What it draws

- **Nodes are notes.** A written note is a filled sphere. A red link, a concept named but not yet written, is a **frontier node**. It is drawn receded and never hidden by default, because the frontier is what the memory is growing toward, and a map that hid it would misstate the shape. Skill maps are not nodes. Arrangement is a lens drawn on the graph (below), never a thing in it.
- **Edges are wikilinks**, one per linked pair. Links in the memory have a direction, and backlinks and unresolved references depend on it, but the whole graph reads better undirected, so arrowheads are off by default and one toggle turns them on. _(2026-09-26: at rest the edges are invisible, so nothing crosses a name; later that day faint-at-rest was tried and rejected because even a faint web makes labels hard to read. Hovering or picking a note lights the edges that touch it, and only those. The turn's story lights transiently: the links the turn adds or breaks, and the links a note arriving in the reading makes to the notes already there, are lit while the turn runs and for a moment after it lands, then fall back to nothing. A note the turn worked glows, but its existing links stay dark. *(Corrected later on 2026-09-26: the first proof of concept lit every link of every worked note and held them at rest, which buried the labels; the story is the links being made, not the neighbourhood a note already had.)* So the web stays legible while notes move, and a link the agent makes lights where it lands, which is what the thread's reading is for. Adopted from the dictionary, where the same rule lets nodes drift without the lines tangling. Nodes keep clear of each other: their size stays small against the distance between linked notes, and the layout keeps a margin around each.)_
- **Labels stay on the map.** Every node carries its name, fading with distance from the camera and returning as it comes closer. This departs from the [[Checkpoint Graph]]'s rule that no node carries text, deliberately: a checkpoint's identity is a message, which no map can carry, while a note's identity is its name ([[Memory]], "names are the identity"). A graph of notes without names shows nothing. Everything beyond the name is in the popover.
- **Size is connectedness.** The more links meet at a note, the bigger it draws, so hubs read as hubs, the rule the [[Checkpoint Graph]] uses for junctions.
- **The shape is a web in space.** A memory links in cycles, so it takes the relational reading on [[Skill Maps]], settled by forces rather than ranked, and the forces settle in three dimensions. Depth gives a large memory room that a flat web does not have: clusters sit apart instead of on top of each other. You turn the graph by dragging and zoom toward the pointer. _(2026-09-26: the graph opens, and Fit returns, at one reading density, links a few label heights long, centred on the reading, never the whole memory pressed into the frame. A large memory is read by moving through it at that density; what is beyond the frame is not drawn.)_ _(2026-09-30: two refinements. The graph draws only the reading: the notes the turns touched, their neighbours receded, and what the reader's hand adds; the whole memory is data the picture never pays for per frame, and Whole memory is the opt-in that does. And the graph never zooms out past reading density to fit anything: a checkpoint lands centred on the notes the turn touched now, and a reading larger than the frame, as a code turn's is, extends beyond it and is reached by panning. Seen first on the [[Code Graph]], where a turn's reading is hundreds of symbols and fitting it pressed them to dots.)_

## Surfacing with the turn

**Why it exists.** Thinking with an agent over the memory, the ideas arrive in the conversation but land in notes, and the notes are atomic, one concept each, arranged only by their links. To keep that structure in mind while deciding the next message (which notes the agent is in, what they link to, where a new link went), a person leaves the conversation for Obsidian, searches for each note the agent touched, and opens its local graph. The thread's reading makes the trip unnecessary: the notes being worked on and their neighborhoods assemble beside the thread as the agent works. _(2026-09-25.)_

**What surfaces.** The reading starts from the note or skill map the thread's first turn began with, or empty if it began with neither. Then, while each turn runs:

- **A note the agent reads rises into view** and glows, bringing its neighbors with it, receded, so each worked note arrives with its local graph. The reading is the union of those local graphs: the notes being worked on in front, their surroundings behind. How far a neighborhood reaches is one setting, one link by default, the way Obsidian's local graph has a depth.
- **A note the agent amends is marked.** A note it writes for the first time arrives as new. When the new note fills a red link, the frontier node turns written where it stands, so a filled red link reads as a change, not as a stranger arriving.
- **A link the agent adds draws in as it lands**, marked new, between the two notes it joins, and brings the far note into view if it was not already. **A link the agent removes draws broken** where it stood, the same mark a lens uses for a vanished edge. Links are the memory's structure, so a turn that changes them has changed what the person is holding in mind, and the graph shows the change itself, not only the notes around it.
- **A red link the agent reaches toward** appears as a frontier node.

The graph follows the turn live, from the same activity the conversation's work log shows. Once the turn ends, it shows what the turn's record kept: its grounding ([[Assistant]]) and its amendments ([[Memory]]). The graph never says more than the record does.

**Following the agent.** When a note rises, the graph turns just enough to bring it forward, then stops. Once you turn or zoom the graph yourself, it stops following, says so, and offers one control that resumes following, the way a thread's conversation stays pinned to the newest content until you scroll away ([[T3code Thread View]]). The graph never takes the camera back from you.

**Stepping through the thread.** The reading holds everything the thread has reached, not only the latest turn: the current turn's notes and links in front, earlier turns' settled behind them. Picking a checkpoint, in the conversation or in the thread's [[Checkpoint Graph|Checkpoints]], moves the graph to that point: it shows what the thread had reached by then, with that turn's notes and links in front. The graph has no timeline of its own; the thread's checkpoints are its history. _(Amended 2026-09-26: the reading gathers its notes to the front.)_ The notes the reading holds are drawn together into one cluster in front of you, wherever they rest in the memory, so a note the agent reaches from the far side of the memory comes into view and the links it makes are drawn between notes close enough to read. This is what invisible edges buy: notes can move freely without the web tangling. Each note keeps a home, its place in the memory's layout, and stability is measured against homes and against the cluster: between checkpoints the cluster grows, new arrivals settling at its edge while the notes already in it stay where they were, so stepping through the thread still shows the structure growing rather than reshuffling. When the reading ends, or you ask for the whole memory, every note springs home. Given up: absolute places for the notes in a reading, which had kept the picture stable but pinned the story to wherever the memory happened to put its notes. One toggle narrows the reading to the turn at the checkpoint you are on. Nothing is stored for any of this: each checkpoint's reading is derived from the records of the turns up to it, like the layout.

Picking a node yourself does the same: its neighbors come forward and turn toward you, the way the dictionary surfaces related terms. The whole memory is one toggle away, and it is the memory as the thread's branch has it, so nothing beside a thread mixes in another version.

## Storytelling

The graph tells the story of a turn, and a story has levels. _(Recorded 2026-09-26, Venkat's ruling after the proof of concept lit every neighbour alike.)_

- **At rest, nothing.** No edge is drawn. The notes stand where they are, labels clear.
- **The reader's hand never moves a note.** _(Ruled 2026-09-27.)_ Hovering, picking, and choosing a move change ink, size, labels, and lit links only. A note's place changes for one reason: the reading changed. The dictionary pulls a focused note's neighbours toward it with a little jitter; tried here, it read as the graph flinching at the pointer. Given up: the focus pull.
- **The neighbourhood stays dark.** _(Ruled 2026-09-27; it first lit faintly.)_ When the turn surfaces notes and they gather to the front, the links they already had to the notes around them are not drawn. Lighting a note's links is what hovering does, and it belongs to the reader's hand alone: when the story drives the view, that same look is noise that pulls the eye from what the turn made. Given up: faint context links during a turn. Where an arrival sits is told by where it lands, not by lines.
- **What the turn made is the story, and it is the most prominent thing on screen.** A link the turn added draws at full ink, and **beads travel along it** in the link's direction: from the note whose prose links out, to the note it names. A link added in both directions carries beads both ways. A link the turn removed draws broken, still. The notes the turn worked, and both ends of every link it added, are drawn at full ink on top of everything, **larger than their neighbours, with larger labels** _(Venkat, 2026-09-26: the ring alone is not enough; size is what the eye reads first)_, and their **labels are outlined in paper** and exempt from the depth fade, so the names in the story read over any line that crosses them. Hovered and picked labels get the same outline.
- **A checkpoint is a series of moves, and the graph shows them one at a time.** _(Venkat, 2026-09-26.)_ A turn reads notes, writes a new one, amends one, adds a link, removes a link, in order. Beside the graph, each checkpoint has a **play-by-play**: one line per move, in the order the agent made them, in plain words ("read Atlas Graph", "wrote PR Review", "linked PR Review to Merges", "unlinked Publishing from Checkpoint Graph"). Clicking a play shows that move on the graph and nothing else: a read, a write, or an amendment lights its note, full ink and outlined label, and turns the camera to it; a link lights both notes and the edge between them, beads running; a removed link lights both notes and the broken edge. The rest of the story steps back to the neighbourhood level while a play is chosen, and the chosen play holds, like a picked note, until it is clicked again, another play is clicked, Escape is pressed, or the checkpoint changes. The play-by-play is the story in words; the graph is the same story in space, and each line is a door between them. **Playback walks the play-by-play** _(Venkat, 2026-09-26)_: pressing play steps through each checkpoint's moves in order, holding on each long enough to read it and, for a link, to see the beads run, then lets the turn's story settle and moves to the next checkpoint. Pausing stops on the move it is showing; clicking a move by hand pauses playback.
- **The agent has a cursor on the map, and each move is a gesture.** _(Venkat, 2026-10-01: links were easy to follow, since they have a colour, a direction, and a motion that takes time; notes only grew, which says "important" rather than what happened, and says it all at once.)_ The editor's agent cursor ([[Atlas Editor]]) has a twin on the graph: a small marker that visits the turn's moves in the order the agent made them, pausing on each, while the play-by-play row lights in step. _(2026-10-02: it first left a fading trail of attention behind it; with the chip naming each move, the trail said nothing the chip did not, and it is gone.)_ When the cursor reaches a note, the note makes one short gesture for its verb and stops: a **written** note draws itself into existence, a ring closing into a filled dot with one outward ripple; an **amended** note takes a single sweep around it and its fill refreshes; a **read** note gets a soft glance, a halo that brightens and fades, no ink change; a **removed** note drains to an outline that then fades. Gestures are transient and sequential, so by the turn's end the reader has watched each note's fate. Growth is no longer the story's: it stays with hover, and size means connectedness again. _(Venkat, 2026-10-01, after the first cursor: it was too small, it vanished and reappeared, and it sat on top of the notes it visited.)_ **The cursor is never absent.** When it has nothing to do, at rest, after a turn falls, or before the first move, it parks in a corner of the canvas and waits there; it travels from the corner to the first move and back to the corner at the end, so it never pops in or out. **It never hurries.** Every travel takes its full eased time; a long turn is told at the level the span chose, one visit per shown thing, with the moves inside one thing told as one visit. **It never covers a note.** It stops beside the note it visits, pointing at it, and rides a link just short of the far note's rim. **It is large enough to follow**, drawn in the story colour with a light outline so it reads over notes and lines. **It carries a floating label** that says what is happening in the words of the play-by-play, "wrote PR Review", "linked PR Review to Merges", styled like the lit row, updated as each travel begins so the reader knows where it is going.
- **The glyph in the list is the glyph on the map.** A small glyph before each name in the play-by-play, a plus for written, a tilde for amended, nothing for read, is the same glyph on the note's label for the rest of the checkpoint, so "which one was that" is answered by looking.
- **Then it falls, and leaves a quiet mark.** Beads stop, made links and the neighbourhood ease back to nothing, the story nodes return to the reading's ordinary ink; but the notes this checkpoint worked keep their label glyph and a thin ring in the story colour until the reader steps, the note's counterpart to a removed link drawing broken, so at rest the checkpoint still says what it changed. The graph is still again; the story is what you saw while the agent worked.

Adopted from the AI Coding Dictionary, whose focused node sends beads down its links and outlines its labels. There the beads always run outward from the focused node; here they run with the link's direction, because incoming and outgoing links mean different things in a memory. This is the payoff of invisible edges ([[Atlas Graph#What it draws|What it draws]]): with nothing drawn at rest, a note can travel in from the far side of the memory and the links it makes are the only lines you see.

## Lenses: skill maps drawn on the graph

Obsidian colors groups by search query. Here the groups already exist. Turning on a [[Skill Maps|skill map]] as a **lens** tints the notes and edges the map arranges, and hovering one of its edges names the edge's type in the map's own vocabulary, so the graph shows what the map covers and what it leaves out. Notes no map places stay plain, so what has no map becomes visible at a glance. A map's standing warnings draw as **broken edges**: an edge whose prose link vanished, marked where it stood ([[Memory]], "Staleness"). Several lenses may show at once, each in its own tint. In the thread's reading, the skill map the turn invoked, or the one whose territory its first grounding touched, turns on as a lens by itself ([[Atlas]]), and you can turn it off like any other.

## Filters

A search narrows the graph to matching notes and their neighbors. Toggles hide frontier nodes, hide **orphans** (notes with no link in either direction, which the graph cannot reach and which are themselves a finding about the memory), and show or hide each lens. Tags and folders have no filter, because a memory is flat and untagged by design ([[Memory]]).

## Where it opens

The graph is a [[Thread Tabs|thread tab]], opened from the thread's header and linked, like every thread tab, to the tab showing its thread: when that tab moves to another thread, the graph follows, the way Obsidian's local graph follows the note pane it is linked to. It draws the memory as that thread's branch has it, unmerged notes and links included ([[Memory]], "Branches, and the merge home"), and stands in a [[Panes|pane]] beside the conversation. It centers on the note the [[Atlas Editor]] has open when you are editing one. Incoming and outgoing links are each toggleable. It is the [[Atlas]]'s map beside a thread: the skill map the turn works in shows as a lens on it, not as a second layout (resolved below).

## Acts

- **Clicking a written node opens the note** in the [[Atlas Editor]]. Hover brings a node and its links forward while the rest recedes. A hover that lingers summons the popover: the name, links in and out counted, the maps that place it, the red links it names, and when it last changed. This is the same two-stage reading the Checkpoint Graph resolved.
- **Clicking a frontier node opens the frontier door** ([[Memory]]): a seeded message that starts an amendment to write the note, staged in the [[Composer]] for the thread the composer names. The memory's one write path is a turn, never an editor, and the graph only shows the memory.
- **Dragging a node** moves it for this look only. Positions are derived, never stored: the layout is part of the index, and the index is cache ([[Memory]]).

## Settings

Node size, line thickness, label fade, and the forces (center, repel, link distance) are facts about you, and they persist across memories like the [[Checkpoint Graph]]'s display settings. Which lenses and filters are on persists per memory, because which maps matter is a fact about the memory.

## Open Decisions

### Is the graph two-dimensional?

_(Opened 2026-09-26.)_ The note designs the picture in three dimensions, after the AI Coding Dictionary. After using the proof of concept with both readings, Venkat found the flat reading the better, more useful experience: names read without depth in the way, the gathering is plainer, nothing hides behind anything. If depth carries no meaning here, the third dimension is cost without story. **Three-dimensional, as designed:** the memory as a cloud, with depth fade and a turning camera. **Two-dimensional only:** one flat web, the same rules, no depth. **Recommendation:** none yet. Two more readings are being built to compare against the existing flat one: a stripped-down flat renderer on the same engine, and an equivalent flat renderer on a two-dimensional engine (Pixi), so the choice can be made by using them and by measuring, not by argument. Given up if two-dimensional wins: the cloud, the turn of the camera toward a risen note, and depth as a cue for distance from the reading.

**Resolved (2026-10-23): two-dimensional only.** Venkat ruled the Graph tab flat, drawn by the stripped-down flat renderer on the same engine: the Lean renderer, on three.js with troika labels, which the proof of concept has drawn with by default since 2026-10-02. The three-dimensional renderer, the Pixi renderer and the earlier flat renderer leave the tab. Why: the flat reading was the better experience in use (above), and Lean draws it on the engine the graph already has. Given up: the cloud, the turn of the camera toward a risen note, depth as a cue for distance from the reading, and a flat renderer on a two-dimensional engine of its own (Pixi).

### How may the graph move?

_(Raised 2026-09-24.)_ The dictionary's graph turns slowly on its own. The product-wide rule is that nothing moves while you are at rest ([[Visual Language]]), and a view that keeps redrawing uses the graphics processor for as long as it is on screen, which is one of the costs this product refuses. **Always turning:** the graph keeps rotating, which reads as alive and shows its depth, at a steady cost for as long as the tab is open. **Moving with the turn:** the graph moves only when a note rises into view or you pick one, turning just enough to bring it forward and then stopping, and you turn it yourself by dragging. At rest it is still. **Recommendation:** moving with the turn. The motion then means something, a note has entered the agent's reach, and the rule on rest holds. Given up: a graph that turns while nothing is happening.

**Resolved (2026-09-26): moving with the turn.** At rest the graph is still. It moves only when a note comes into view or you pick one, turning just enough to bring it forward, and otherwise only when you drag it. The motion carries meaning, a note has entered the agent's reach, and the product's rule that nothing moves at rest holds ([[Visual Language]]). The camera that follows the agent until you take it (above) depends on this. Given up: a graph that turns while nothing is happening.

**Refined (2026-09-26): still at rest, organic in motion, breathing while the agent works.** The graph never moves at rest. Two exceptions carry meaning. Every transition moves organically: a note rising, neighbors drawn in, a settle after a step, each note at its own spring speed with a slight drift as it lands, and a link landing sends a brief wave down the edges it touches. _(Breathing while a turn runs, a slow drift with a faint pulse in size, was tried and given up on 2026-09-27: it made hovering a moving target and cost frames for the whole turn. The graph is still whenever nothing is transitioning, agent working or not.)_ Given up: the dictionary's always-on drift and idle rotation, which the 2026-09-26 proof of concept showed to be the pleasant part of its feel, deliberately traded for a graph whose motion always means something.

### Does the whole-memory reading show branches?

Inside a thread the memory is the thread's branch ([[Memory]], "Branches, and the merge home"); the Memory page stands outside every thread. As **main only**, the page draws shared truth and nothing else: simple and never surprising, at the cost of never seeing an unmerged branch's shape except from inside its thread. As **main with branches overlaid**, the page draws each branch's new notes and filled red links distinctly over main, the merge home's walk as a picture, at the cost of one graph carrying several truths at once. **Recommendation:** main only on the page; a branch is seen inside its thread, where the thread's reading already lights from the record. Given up: every branch's frontier work in one picture. The thread rows and the memory tab already say which threads amended.

**Resolved (2026-09-26): main only.** The Memory page draws shared truth and nothing else. A branch's shape is seen inside its thread, where the thread's reading steps through every checkpoint of the work. Declined as well: a picker on the page that switches the graph to one branch, which kept truths apart but added a control for something the thread already shows. Given up: every branch's frontier work in one picture.

_(Superseded later on 2026-09-26: the Memory page's graph was dropped, so the question no longer arises. The graph opens only beside a thread and draws that thread's branch. A whole-memory graph outside any thread is declined for now: the graph's job is keeping the structure in view while you think with an agent, and the Memory page keeps its list, search, and red links for browsing. Given up: seeing the whole memory's shape without opening a thread.)_

### Are the thread's reading and the Atlas map one tab or two?

The [[Atlas]] map shows the skill map a turn is working in, lit by the turn. The thread's reading here shows every note the thread's turns reach, whatever map places it. **One tab, two readings:** a single map tab beside the thread switches between the skill map and the whole graph, under one camera. **Two tabs:** each stands on its own, and splits can show them side by side. **Recommendation:** one tab, two readings. Both are the memory drawn spatially and lit by the same record, and a second tab would compete with the conversation for room. Given up: the skill map and the whole graph on screen at once.

**Resolved (2026-09-26): one tab, and the skill map is a lens on it.** Neither option as framed. Beside a thread there is one graph, the thread's reading, and the skill map the turn works in turns on as a lens: its notes and edges tinted, each edge's type named on hover in the map's own vocabulary. The Atlas map pane is not a separate layout; the thread's reading is that pane. Why: once the reading shows every note the thread reaches with its local graph, and lenses already draw a map's coverage on the graph, a second layout of the same record beside the thread adds a mode switch and says nothing new. Given up: a map's declared shape (tree, flow, or web) beside a thread. That shape is drawn where a map is read on its own ([[Skill Maps]]).

### Does the turn's reading hold one turn, or the thread?

_(Raised 2026-09-25.)_ A conversation that designs something runs many turns over the same notes, and the structure worth keeping in mind is the conversation's, not only the latest reply's. **One turn:** the reading clears when a turn starts and shows only what that turn reached. It stays small and needs no narrowing, but every message wipes the picture the person was using to write it. **The thread:** the reading holds every note and link the thread has reached, the current turn's in front and earlier turns' settled behind them, derived from each turn's record along the thread's line ([[Threads]]). **Recommendation:** the thread, and the reading is renamed the thread's reading. It is what the trip to Obsidian was for: the notes the whole conversation has been working on, not just those since the last message. A thread is one line, so what it has reached is well defined. Given up: a picture that stays small on its own. A long thread's reading grows, and a "this turn only" toggle and the filters do the narrowing.

**Resolved (2026-09-26): the thread, stepped through by its checkpoints.** Venkat proposed the middle ground: keep each turn's picture and let the person return to any of them. Each turn already ends in a checkpoint and the graph derives from the record, so every turn's picture can be rebuilt without storing anything. Taken with one refinement: a checkpoint shows everything the thread had reached by then, with that turn in front, because a picture of one turn at a time would leave the person assembling the conversation's structure across steps, which is the trip to Obsidian again. The reading is renamed the thread's reading. Navigation uses the thread's own checkpoints rather than a timeline of the graph's own, so there is one history, and notes keep their places between checkpoints, so stepping shows growth rather than a reshuffle. A "this turn only" toggle keeps the narrow picture one click away. Given up: a reading that stays small without asking.
