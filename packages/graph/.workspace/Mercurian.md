# Mercurian

Mercurian is a research partner for engineers who direct coding agents: people who research, fork, parallelize, and review work rather than type every line, on their own machines, against repositories they own. Teams where each person drives several agents at once are the first audience; the solo builder with an ambitious project is the second. Work organizes as [[Threads|threads]]. Your own are your projects on your computers and need no account; people working together form [[Teams|teams]] inside [[Organizations|organizations]], a team being a set of repositories its members work across, and publish threads into them. Each thread is one conversation with an [[Assistant|assistant]] and one line of checkpoints — threads fork into threads and take context from one another, forming the project's [[DAG]] — grounded in its repositories and its [[Memory|memory]], changing code in its home repository (how far a thread reaches past it is open on [[Teams]]), and returning what was learned to memory as reviewed amendments.

Its contained concepts are [[Left Sidebar]], [[Mobile App]].

This is the root of the vault's containment spine. The [[Left Sidebar]] is how you navigate, and its contents contain everything else — in particular [[Teams]] and [[Threads]], the surface where the product happens.

The product-wide [[Visual Language]] gives those surfaces one expression without pretending that appearance is another place in the navigation. The [[Design Lab]] — likewise off the spine, and present only in development builds — is the instrument that language is evolved with, on the running product. The [[Marketing Site]] — also off the spine — is the product's public face, built to show the product's own surfaces live rather than pictures of them.

## Open Decisions

### Link style: wikilinks vs. standard markdown links (vault-wide)

(Moved from [[Left Sidebar]] when this root note was created.) **Resolved (2026-07): wikilinks — de facto adopted since the vault began, now recorded as settled.** The original recommendation: They're Obsidian-native (red links for unwritten notes — which this vault relies on — plus rename propagation and graph view), and terser to author. Cost: portability to non-Obsidian tooling. Since this vault is read in Obsidian and by agents that parse the syntax trivially, portability is the cheaper sacrifice. These notes use wikilinks.

**Reasoning amended (2026-08): the outcome stands; the Obsidian-native half of the reasoning is superseded.** [[Memory]] removes Obsidian from consideration entirely — the product neither relies on nor integrates with it, and portability means plain text and git rather than any particular tool. Wikilinks remain settled on what's left of the original reasoning, which is sufficient: they are terse, the red-link convention is load-bearing, and agents parse them trivially. What the editor affordance used to provide — rename propagation — becomes the product's own duty once memory lands (the identity decision on [[Memory]]).

### Is the product plan-first?

From 2026-07 the design carried a document layer beside the conversation: a **plan** (an effort's approach) and a **spec** (its contract, derived from an imported tracker issue), first as commit kinds of their own with dedicated tools, then — 2026-09-03 and 09-04 — as durable project files with a dashboard, freshness statuses (_plan may be stale_, _stale spec_), an implement gate with per-repository splits, and review comments anchored to document text. The root of this note read "Mercurian turns engineering issues into plans, and plans into working code."

**Resolved (2026-09-16): no. Plan-first retires whole; research is the product's verb.** Plan-first encourages a bad habit: engineers generate a plan and execute it without reading or understanding it. Planning is better done interactively, step by step, in the thread itself — where every step is a checkpoint that can be forked, compared, and reviewed. What Mercurian is for is research: finding out, in conversation with an assistant grounded in real repositories and standing memory, what is true and what to do; forking to try several answers; running them in parallel; and reviewing what came back. The checkpoint system and memory are kept whole, because they are what make that loop legible and durable. What retires is every product-owned document the loop was made to produce: plans, specs, the dashboard over them, freshness as a status, the implement gate, issue import as a thread's birth, and document-anchored review comments. Agents still write files — Markdown included — in the repositories they work in; the product knows nothing of their genre, the same content-blind rule [[Memory]] already keeps.

The retired notes were **deleted, not kept as superseded records**, because every amendment is a commit and git holds every version: Plans, Specs, Plan Dashboard, Issue Planning Flow, Issues, Issue Import, Trackers, Issue Status, and the earlier records Coding Sessions, Coding Session View, Splits, Technical Plans, Issue Revisions, Repository Filter, Dashboard, Concepts, and AI Provider Architecture. A read tool cannot see a deleted note, so this entry is the pointer: the design history is in the vault's git history at and before this date. Given up: durable approach and contract documents the product understands; a thread born from a tracker issue; anything a reviewer could learn from a superseded record without opening history. Kept: threads as the unit of work with one conversation and one branching history; forks and merges human-driven only; the working-state pool and snapshot chain; memory as project-scoped standing knowledge amended on the line and merged home by a person. _(2026-09-24: the branching moved from inside the thread to the [[DAG]] of threads, and a line is a thread — [[Threads]].)_

Open, to be designed from use rather than assumed: what research looks like as a surface beyond the conversation and its checkpoints _(designed 2026-09-24 on [[Atlas]])_; whether and how a thread can start from a tracker issue, now that nothing is derived from one; and how a team works in one project — the first audience — against [[Publishing]]'s local-first, publish-is-an-act posture.
