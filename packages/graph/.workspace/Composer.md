# Composer

The composer is where you act in a [[Threads|thread]]: every message you send from it becomes a checkpoint on the thread's one line of history ([[Threads]]). Since "forks and transfers are human-driven only" ([[DAG]]), and a fork opens with a message, the composer is also where every fork begins — and where a transfer is staged ([[Merges]]). One composer sends every kind of turn — conversation, research, building — with no mode to enter; beside the model picker it carries the **runtime mode**, which governs working-tree actions only ([[Assistant]], [[Threads]]).

## One composer for every pane

The app has one composer, and it serves whichever [[Panes|pane]] has focus in the [[Tabs|tab]]. The thread it will send to is named by the focused pane's header, with the thread's attention mark ([[Panes]]) _(2026-09-30, Venkat; a caption above the input carried it from 2026-09-28)_. In a thread's tab, that is the thread. In a [[Spaces|space]] of several, the pane header's title is a switcher listing the space's threads; choosing one brings its conversation into that pane _(2026-09-29, Venkat; moved from the composer's caption 2026-09-30)_. In a thread's local DAG, it is the thread of the checkpoint you picked; sending from a checkpoint that is not its thread's latest forks a new thread, as continuing from an earlier checkpoint always does ([[Checkpoint Graph]]). In a note, a graph, or a page, it is the thread the tab was opened from, or the thread you last had in focus when there is none. Moving focus changes the composer's thread and swaps in that thread's draft, since drafts stay with their thread (below).

Hovering the composer shows the last turn of the thread it names, just above it, so you can see what you are replying to without leaving the pane you are in. Clicking that preview opens the thread in a tab, or focuses its tab when it is already open. _(2026-09-24: adapted from the Codex app's composer for its side pane. Given up: a composer inside each thread's own space, and typing into two threads without moving focus between them.)_

## Suggested next messages

Standing forks the project's [[Memory|memory]] carries — a note's Open Decisions — surface as **suggested next messages** in the composer when the conversation touches the note that carries them. Choosing one appends an editable suggestion to the bottom of the current thread's draft, separated from existing text by a line break; an empty draft receives only the suggestion, and existing text, attachments, and pending review comments stay intact. Only sending makes it a turn. Suggestions are offers, not actions — nothing happens until you send.

## The model picker

The composer carries the thread's model picker: which [[Providers|provider and model]] the [[Assistant|assistant]] runs under. It is live from the first draft message: a new thread starts under the pair you last ran under, and flipping the picker before you send is how a thread starts under a different provider, with no creation step in the way (a thread has no creation moment: "nothing exists until its first commit," [[Threads]]). The choice is a fact of the thread's record, not a knob on the thread: each message that opens a turn records what it ran under, and the picker shows the choice in effect at the tip — so a forked thread inherits the choice at its origin checkpoint (the 2026-08 amendments on [[Assistant]]; their "per branch" reads "per thread" since 2026-09-24, [[Threads]]).

The effective choice is switchable between turns, across providers as well as models; there is no provider-family lock after the first message (the 2026-08 resolution on [[Assistant]]). Flipping the picker is draft-local: nothing lands until your next message, which records the provider and model it ran under — there is no switch commit. Changing it never changes a reply already in progress: that reply finishes under the provider and model it started with, while the next reply begins a fresh assistant session reconstructed from the thread's recorded history. Each assistant commit records the provider and model that produced it. The picker speaks provider and model abstractly, never an instance — each machine resolves the pair to its own ([[Providers]]).

_(2026-09-03: the composer is the adopted thread view's composer, unchanged — [[Threads]]. Its picker chooses per thread, so the record above is what the adopted control produces rather than a Mercurian addition; a reply continues its thread's session, and only a forked thread's first reply begins a session carried from its origin — natively or rebuilt, [[Threads]].)_

## When the assistant can't reply

Sending is unavailable when the thread's model can't run a turn on this machine, and the composer says which of four things is true rather than failing quietly: **no model is chosen yet** — none of your threads has ever run a turn; **the provider isn't on this machine**; **the provider is here but you aren't signed in** (the 2026-08 resolution on [[Providers]]); or **the model isn't available on this machine's instance** — which, when an agent is simply too old, names the upgrade that unlocks it. Each states where to fix it, and none of them ever rewrites the saved choice: the pair stays visible, and the same thread resolves fine on a machine that has what it names.

Two things deliberately stay open. **Drafts don't block** — a thread is born by its first message whether or not an assistant can answer it ([[Threads]]), so the new-thread composer shows the same sentence as information rather than a refusal. And **a message that lands, lands**: sending is never conditional on the reply, so a window that raced a sign-out or a settings change still commits its message, and the notice explaining why nothing is streaming arrives after it. The gate is the composer speaking first, not the history refusing.

## Acting from anywhere in the history

The composer acts from wherever you stand. Normally that's the tip of the conversation you're in; after picking an earlier continuable checkpoint in the [[Checkpoint Graph]], sending continues from that checkpoint's terminal state — after the assistant response and therefore after everything the turn landed. The Checkpoint Graph is where you choose the coherent state; the composer is where you act from it.

## Editing an earlier message

_(2026-09-03: on the thread space this is the **Fork here** action on any message of yours — the adopted view's revert slot, made ours; [[Threads]].)_

A human message is primarily something to **edit and branch**, not a state to append another human message after. Choosing that act copies its text and attachments into the composer of a new thread forked from the checkpoint before the original message. Sending makes that thread real with the edited query as its first message; the original thread is untouched. It never rewrites the old message.

The composer makes the consequence explicit while editing: **Sending creates a new thread; the original remains unchanged.** (The separate **continue exactly here** act, which lived only in the detailed commit reading, went with that reading — dropped 2026-08. A human query is never an ordinary continuation point.)

## Composing

- **Grounding mentions.** While typing, you can mention a file from the project's repositories — or a note from the project's [[Memory|memory]] — by name; the mention becomes a chip in the message and travels with it, so the [[Assistant|assistant]] grounds its reply in exactly what you pointed at.
- **Images.** Messages can carry image attachments — screenshots and diagrams arrive with issues naturally.
- **Send and stop.** The composer's send control, and the stop control while the thread is working, are the identity's dot-and-ring controls drawn bare at 28px with no disc behind them: strokes in the text colour, the pivot dot in the same colour at half strength as the icon's second tone ([[Visual Language]], amended 2026-09-27). Send is disabled while the draft is empty; there is no dictation and no voice state. Queue and steer wait for a running turn.
- **Vim.** The composer can take Vim's modes and motions, off by default and turned on in [[Settings]]. With it on, the composer opens in insert mode, Escape enters normal mode without leaving the composer, and normal mode shows the draft in the monospace font so the mode is visible without a badge; Enter sends only from insert mode. Turning it on asks you to type `:q` first, proof that you know the way out, which replaces any in-composer escape hatch. _(2026-09-27, Venkat: insert by default; "escaping shouldn't unfocus the composer"; the `:q` gate; monospace in normal mode. The same day's "Escape Vim" button was withdrawn for the gate.)_ In normal mode Space is the app's leader key, so the composer joins keyboard navigation with Esc then Space ([[Search Palette]]).
- **Drafts.** An unsent message stays with the thread where you wrote it: leave the thread space — or switch threads — and come back, and it's waiting exactly where it was, never carried into a conversation it wasn't a reply to. A draft rides its thread as the conversation grows under it; one staged by [[#Editing an earlier message|editing an earlier message]] waits at the fork it would open instead. (Narrowed 2026-08 from per-plan: a draft is a reply composed somewhere, and the model flip's where-it-was-made scoping was already the right rule for all of it. 2026-09-03: a thread is the unit the adopted composer keeps drafts by, so this is the adopted rule, not an addition.)

## Slash commands and skills

The composer carries the slash surface of the shell this design adapts: commands and [[Skills|skills]] supplied by the effective provider, invoked while composing ([[T3code Thread View]]). What a machine's installed agent doesn't offer is gated, not failed, and an invocation is part of the message that carries it — recorded in the history like everything else a message stages. Skills are how a team's house style enters the work; the concept, and the open question of whether skills travel with the project, live on [[Skills]].

## The prompt stash

_A deferred idea (2026-08) — recorded, not adopted design._ The composer pattern as built carries a **prompt stash**: a drafted message set aside — text and image attachments — and restorable into any thread's composer later ([[T3code Thread View]]). The idea worth keeping when this returns: one shelf every one of your threads reaches, so a prompt written against one conversation can be carried to another, or to a different provider entirely — which is why a stashed prompt deliberately carries no model choice, and why the stash is the draft's opposite: where a draft stays with the branch it replies to ([[#Composing]]), the stash is unscoped, for prompts meant to travel, personal, and in no history. Deferred rather than designed: the composer reaching the shelf is the new part, and nothing forces the call yet.

## Background sending

_Declined (2026-08)._ The composer pattern as built later grew a modifier on send for brand-new drafts: hold it and the new thread starts in the background while another fresh draft opens at once — a gesture for dispatching several worker threads in a row ([[T3code Thread View]]). Mercurian takes neither half. A thread's first message opens a conversation you join, not a worker you dispatch, and a reply streaming anywhere never holds a composer hostage elsewhere — threads answer at once ([[#While the assistant responds]]). Revisit if people ask to fire off many threads in a row — the first audience drives several agents at once ([[Mercurian]]), so this declined gesture may return sooner than most; until then, a thread's birth stays a deliberate act a background send would blur.

## While the assistant responds

While the [[Assistant|assistant]] is responding in the thread you are in, the send action becomes **Stop** — you can interrupt mid-response, and stopping cuts short only that thread's reply. There is no queueing: one turn at a time _in each thread_, and the next message in a thread waits until its response finishes or you stop it. _(2026-09-24: the adopted orchestrator can steer a running turn in place, restart it with new instructions, or queue a message to run after it; which of these the composer offers here is not re-decided in this pass.)_ (One further wait joined 2026-08-31: a turn claims a worktree slot per repository it touches, and when the pool is at its cap the send waits for a slot and says so — steering a turn already running never waits. [[Threads]].) A reply streaming in another thread never gates you: switch threads and the composer there is live, and replies in different threads run at once, each settling into its own thread. (The composer pattern this adapts, as built, is documented in [[T3code Thread View]]. Narrowed 2026-08 from thread-wide one-turn-at-a-time: t3code has one thread where ours has many branches, and branches are independent everywhere else — each carries its own state and model choice — so serializing them against each other was the shell's shape, not this design's. Given up: the guarantee that at most one reply is ever being written into a thread's history at a time.)

## Open Decisions

### Where does the one composer sit?

_(Raised 2026-09-24.)_ **Beneath the focused pane:** the composer moves to whichever [[Panes|split]] has focus, so where it sits says which pane it serves. **Across the bottom of the main area:** it stays in one place under every split, and only its thread label says where a message will go. **Recommendation:** beneath the focused pane. With two threads side by side, a composer that stays put invites sending to the wrong one. Given up: a composer that never moves.

### Does the composer show how much room the next turn has?

The retired coding session wore a context meter because a session was one live runtime whose context genuinely filled as it worked. As first designed, a thread's conversation had no such runtime to measure: each reply began a fresh assistant session reconstructed from the thread's recorded history, and each assistant commit records where that reconstruction stopped being verbatim, keeping the compacted rendition it actually used folded on the commit ([[Assistant]]). A transplanted session meter would therefore measure nothing that exists here.

But the reconstruction itself has a size, and it is knowable before you send: standing anywhere in the history, how much of the effective model's window the record up to this point consumes — equivalently, how close this branch is to replies that lean on summarized rather than verbatim history. That is a real signal with real moves attached: it says where a [[Merges|merge]] should land — the line you stand on keeps its weight while the folded branch arrives compacted, so it pays to stand on the lighter line when either could carry — it says when a fresh fork travels lighter, and it explains a reply that seems to have forgotten something distant. It is honestly model-relative — the same position measures differently under a different model choice, which is itself information a picker flip can use.

The cost: the meter surfaces machinery the design deliberately keeps quiet. Reconstruction is recorded with folded honesty — on the commit, expandable, never announced — and an ambient meter promotes it to standing status. It would also be the first composer element whose value derives from your position and model choice rather than from anything you staged.

**Recommendation:** adopt, in the folded-honesty temperament — a quiet, position-derived meter on the composer, measuring the reconstruction of where you stand under the effective model choice, informational only: never an alarm, and never an act. Raised rather than adopted because it makes ambient something the design chose to keep folded.

**Resolved (2026-08): adopted as recommended.** The composer carries a quiet, position-derived meter measuring the reconstruction of where you stand under the effective model choice. It moves with you — branch switches, checkpoint picks, picker flips — and says when the next reply will lean on summarized rather than verbatim history; with no effective model to measure against it shows nothing rather than a guess. It never blocks sending, never acts on its own, and never appears in the history. Given up: keeping reconstruction entirely folded — the meter makes ambient what was previously visible only on the commit that used it.

**Amended (2026-08): the warning folds, and the meter stands by send.** Two presentation corrections from the first build. The meter sits beside the send control — the position the adopted composer's meter occupies, one meter grammar — not among the pickers. And the warning stays folded: when the next reply would lean on summarized history, the meter wears a small warning mark on the gauge itself, and the sentence saying so lives in the meter's popover — the composer never grows a standing line of warning text. Folded honesty extends to the warning: the glance says something is worth knowing; the popover says what.

**Amended (2026-09-03): the reconstruction meter retires; the context meter stays.** With one thread per line ([[Threads]]), a reply continues its line's live session, and the adopted context meter measures exactly that. Reconstruction happens only at a forked line's first turn, and the rebuilt session is what the context meter then reports. The standing reason for a second meter — every reply rebuilt from the record — is gone, and the composer carries one meter, the adopted one. Given up: the position-derived reading before you send; where a fork would travel lighter is no longer shown on the composer, only recorded on the fork's first checkpoint.
