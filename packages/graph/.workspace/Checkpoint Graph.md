# Checkpoint Graph

The surface for seeing and moving through a thread's history and the family of threads around it — the project's [[DAG]], each thread a single line of checkpoints, forks and transfers the edges between them — read as checkpoints. It is one of the thread's [[Thread Tabs|thread tabs]], labelled **Checkpoints**, opened beside the thread in a split (2026-09-24; formerly the right sidebar's one pinned surface, always present and never closable, 2026-09-03; before that, sharing a pane with the plan artifact behind corner icons). Here you see where threads forked and where context crossed between them, follow any thread, and choose a coherent checkpoint as the place to continue from. _(2026-09-24: formerly the branching, merging history inside one thread — [[Threads]], "History: one line per thread". The views, the reading, and the popover carry over; what they lay out is now a family of threads.)_ (Renamed 2026-08: formerly the **DAG Explorer**, labeled **History** in the pane. The checkpoint is the unit the surface shows, so the name says so. Renamed again 2026-09-02: the product labels the surface **Checkpoints** — on the corner icon and its tooltip, the pane's title, and wherever the app names it — because what you open is the checkpoints, and "graph" named only one of its three views. This note keeps the concept's name; the label is the product's.)

## Three views

The Checkpoint Graph offers three views, switchable inside the open sidebar:

- **Thread** — the reading of where you stand, and the default: the path from the family's root through each origin checkpoint to the tip of the thread you are in, read as one line. Divergence never hides — where other threads forked from a checkpoint on this path, an always-visible badge shows them, and choosing one moves you to that thread's tip; where a checkpoint on this path took context from another thread, the badge names the source, and choosing it re-roots what you're reading without moving you. This is the view for walking the path you're on.
- **Columns** — the Thread's fork choices held open as adjacent segments. Choosing a sibling thread at a fork replaces only the columns beyond it, keeping the checkpoint that produced the divergence visible while you compare where each thread goes. It appears only once the family has a fork worth holding open.
- **Graph** — the spatial map: the whole family laid out as nodes and edges in space, every fork and every transfer visible at once. This is the view for seeing the family's _shape_, not for walking it.

(Re-scoped 2026-08: the git-graph reading, first written up here as the Graph view, became the **navigator** — rows in time order are the easier thing to navigate. The Graph view is the spatial map of the DAG. Formerly the navigator was described as a compact tree-style list; no view renders tree-style now. Re-scoped again later that month: the Navigator's all-branches rail-list is retired for the **Thread** — the walking view shows one checked-out path and surfaces divergence as badges, rather than showing every branch at once.)

**The view selection persists.** It survives closing and reopening the Checkpoints tab and follows you across threads — which view you prefer is a fact about you, not about the thread. A remembered Columns choice falls back to Thread while the thread has no siblings and returns when a fork makes Columns useful again.

## The local DAG

Any thread can open its **local DAG**: the Graph view in a [[Panes|pane]] of its own, the way Obsidian opens a note's local graph. It draws the entire [[DAG]] the thread belongs to, meaning every thread connected to it by a fork or a transfer, and every one of their turns, one node per turn. A spawned thread's turns stay folded under the turn that spawned it, as everywhere on this graph. It opens from the thread's tab, its header menu, and its row's menu in the [[Left Sidebar]]. The tab keeps the view's reading, settings, and acts: hovering emphasizes lineage, a lingering hover shows the popover, and picking a node makes it the checkpoint the [[Composer|composer]] continues from and moves the thread's tab to it when that tab is open. The Checkpoints tab beside a thread can show the same DAG in its Graph view; the local DAG is that view standing on its own.

_(2026-09-24.)_ Declined: a map of every thread at once, each thread one node. A thread-sized node hides the turns where forks start and context crosses, and those are what the DAG exists to show. Your list of threads is the [[Left Sidebar]], a team's is its board ([[Collaborative DAG]]), and each thread's family is its local DAG.

## Continuable checkpoints

The record beneath is finer than the surface — a turn holds its attempts, its tool work, and any child thread it spawned — but the surface's unit, and its only unit, is a **continuable checkpoint**: a coherent product state from which someone is likely to continue. Each turn ends in exactly one.

- A settled assistant turn is one checkpoint. Its human query, every effect the turn landed, and its terminal response appear together. The checkpoint continues from the terminal response, so everything the turn landed is necessarily in its ancestry. A turn that was steered or restarted while running keeps its earlier attempts beneath it, and a child thread an agent spawned nests its own checkpoints beneath the turn that spawned it — neither is a checkpoint of the thread ([[DAG]]).
- The checkpoint distinguishes the query from the response with the conversation's own visual grammar: **You** uses a right-aligned message-bubble glyph, and **Assistant** uses its mirrored left-aligned message-bubble glyph. The glyphs keep their You and Assistant labels wherever there is room, so author remains legible without relying on color alone. The checkpoint also lists its **effects** — what the turn actually landed, derived from the record and never from what the reply says. A turn may carry several at once, or none:
  - **Memory updated** — an amendment landed on the thread's branch of the project's [[Memory|memory]] ([[Threads]], "Memory rides the thread").
  - **Code changed** — read per repository from the snapshot chain: the turn's snapshot differs from the one before it, or the thread's branch moved ([[Threads]], "Every turn ends in a snapshot"). The effect names the repositories it touched.
    A turn with none of these is a **plain turn** — conversation that changed nothing standing. Interrupted is a mark beside the effects, not one of them: a stopped turn keeps whatever it landed before it stopped. So is **steered**: a turn you steered while it ran — by a message, or by an edit to its working state ([[Threads]]) — carries the mark, and its popover shows what it was told; which of its changes were yours is open on [[Threads]]. Response prose never creates an effect badge. _(Amended 2026-09-02, then 2026-09-16: the effects were first plan, spec, interrupted, or no artifact change; building in the same thread ([[Threads]]) added memory and code; the plan-first ruling on [[Mercurian]] removed plan and spec. Two effects remain, and the history still says what each turn did without anyone reading it.)_
- A human message whose turn produced no response is still a checkpoint, honestly marked unanswered. A stopped turn uses its interrupted response as the checkpoint's terminal state.
- Nothing outside a turn is a checkpoint of its own: a transfer rides the turn that took it ([[Merges]]), a fork is another thread's first checkpoint, and the retired design's standalone acts remain readable wherever a history recorded them. _(2026-09-24: formerly a merge was a checkpoint of its own.)_

This is a rendering over the record, not a second history — but it is the only reading. What a checkpoint contains is disclosed by its node popover, never by expanding it into raw commits, and grouping never changes ancestry or continuation semantics.

(Re-scoped 2026-08: the shared **Detail** choice this section once carried — Checkpoints as the default product reading, and a **Commits** audit reading that showed every message and artifact revision independently — is dropped. Checkpoints are the only reading in every view. The commit-level truth stays stored, and nothing about ancestry or continuation changes; given up are the in-product audit reading and, with it, continuing from an intermediate commit.)

## What it shows

- Every turn of every thread in the family remains reachable — plain, memory, code, or a historical kind, yours or the assistant's — through the checkpoint that carries it, and every thread through its origin.
- Which work is published and which is your private, unpublished work (see [[Publishing]]).
- A transfer drawn **once** in every view: on Thread and Columns as a mark on the taking checkpoint naming its source, and in Graph as a dashed edge from the taken checkpoint into the taking one ([[Merges]]). _(2026-09-24: formerly a merge node with an edge from each parent; the tree-style contract that drew a merge under each of its parents retired with it.)_

## How the graph view reads

The map exists to show the history's shape, and its rendering serves that reading:

- **Nodes are glyph discs; status is a corner dot.** Each checkpoint is a disc carrying its kind's glyph — the turn's two-bubble mark, the author-mirrored message bubbles, each standalone act's own symbol — fading to a plain dot when the map is zoomed far out. _(Amended 2026-09-02: a turn's glyph says what the turn did.)_ A plain turn keeps the two-bubble mark; a turn with one effect wears that effect's glyph — the same symbol the standalone act of that kind already carries, so an amendment made inside a turn reads as memory, and a turn that changed code reads as code wherever it sits. A turn with several effects is the fork below. Solid discs are shared history, hollow discs are private work (see [[Publishing]]) — the same distinction Thread and Columns draw. A node's size tells you how connected it is: the more threads fork from a checkpoint or take from it, the bigger it draws, so junctions read as junctions. No node carries a name, message, or any other text on or beside it, at any zoom or selection state; the popover carries identity and detail. What a node _does_ carry beyond its glyph is **status**: small colored fill dots anchored at its top-right corner, stacking when states co-occur, replacing the text badges that once sat beside nodes. _(2026-09-16: the dots carried the retired freshness states — stale spec, stale plan — and, earlier, readiness; with those gone no standing state of the [[Status Vocabulary]] fills the corner today. The corner stays the one place a status may be drawn, so a future state has a home; solid versus hollow discs keep carrying published versus private.)_ Color is an at-a-glance aid, never the only accessible account: every node remains named for assistive technology, statuses included. (Amended 2026-08-19, same day as the resolution below first landed: an earlier wording dropped the glyphs and moved kind color onto the disc itself — a misreading. The glyphs stay; color means status, in the corner.)
- **The map meets your pointer.** Nodes swell gently as the cursor approaches and settle back when it leaves — small targets become easy to pick without the map shouting.
- **Where you stand is emphasized twice.** A ring marks the current commit, and the path through it — the very history the thread space is showing — reads visibly distinct from every other line on the map.
- **Pointing is emphasis.** Hovering a commit brings it and its lineage forward while the rest of the map recedes; moving away restores everything. (This is emphasis in place, not the hover-_preview_ contemplated below — the thread space doesn't move until you pick.)
- **Time reads downward.** Every commit sits beyond its parents along the arrangement's flow, so the direction of history is legible from position alone — edges are straight lines, with no arrowheads, curves, or tapering. One stroke distinction is meaningful: an edge draws **dashed** where what it carries reaches the continuation as compacted summary rather than verbatim — at a [[Merges|merge]], the taking thread's line stays solid and the edge from the taken checkpoint draws dashed. This is the product-wide dashed-context grammar ([[Visual Language]]), not a decoration local to the map.
- **The arrangement is yours to choose.** While Graph is showing, the Checkpoint Graph's settings carry a **Display layout** choice among a few arrangements of the same history — a layered reading, time flowing in ranks, is the default — and **Node size** and **Line thickness** sliders for tuning the drawing. Like the view choice, these persist and follow you across threads: how you like the history drawn is a fact about you, not about the thread.
- **The camera is calm.** Zoom happens where the pointer points and movement eases rather than snaps; it changes only the map's scale and framing, never which checkpoints are shown or what each presents. A commit landing while you read moves the map smoothly to its new arrangement — never a snap, and never at the cost of your pan and zoom. The map never moves on its own while you're at rest, and the same history draws the same picture in every window.
- **The map never loses you.** When the map outgrows the frame, a small overview appears in the corner — the whole shape in miniature, the frame's window marked on it, your position dotted in. Dragging it moves you; when everything fits in view, it gets out of the way.

## The node popover

Every history view — Thread's rows and divergence badges, Columns' segments, Graph's nodes, and any future rendering of the history — carries a **node popover**: the always-available answer to "what is this checkpoint?", without zooming and without navigating. Top to bottom:

- **Identity.** The turn — or a retired kind's standalone act, named by its glyph and name where a history recorded one — and its participants (you or the assistant), relative time, published or private; on a thread's first checkpoint, its origin: the checkpoint it forked from, or the turn that spawned it ([[DAG]]). The provider and model show **once, on the assistant reply**: the pair is _recorded_ on the commit that opens the turn too — that record is what the standing choice and the switch derive from ([[Assistant]]) — but a turn runs under one choice, so saying it beside You as well would only repeat it. (Amended 2026-08-19: first shown on both lines, reading the record too literally.) When a turn's recorded choice differs from its ancestor's, the popover names the switch — a derived reading over the recorded facts, like the checkpoints themselves, never a commit of its own.
- **What changed.** A turn checkpoint shows its query, response, and every effect it carries together — memory and code, the latter per repository with its changed files, branch, and pull-request facts ([[Threads]]) — and, when the turn took context from another thread, the source thread and checkpoint with the compaction folded beneath ([[Merges]]) — so the popover is the full account where the map shows one glyph. Historical commits of retired kinds keep their recorded facts. An assistant commit also carries its reconstruction boundary — where its session's verbatim context began, older history entering as summary — with the compacted rendition folded beneath when one was used ([[Assistant]]).
- **Acts.** Go here (navigate — the resolved selection semantics below). _(The readiness badge and "implement from here" retired with the implement gate, 2026-08-28; building is the [[Composer|composer's]] ordinary next turn.)_

On the graph view this composes with the hover reading: hover still emphasizes lineage in place, and a hover that _lingers_ summons the popover — the _only textual detail_ layer, so pointing stays emphasis and never becomes a modal. **Clicking a node picks it**: the same continue-from-here the popover offers, immediately — selection moves the thread space, exactly the resolved selection semantics below and exactly what clicking a Thread or Columns row does. (Amended 2026-08-19: click first summoned the popover instead of picking; the map's click now acts, and the popover is the hover reading's deliberate second stage. Where a node has no continuation act, click falls back to summoning the popover.) Identity is one linger away at any zoom, leaving the map itself to read as shape, glyphs, and corner status dots.

## What it lets you do

Picking a checkpoint moves you to its terminal state: the thread space follows — opening that checkpoint's thread when it is not the one you are in — showing the complete turn and every effect that turn landed. Continuing from an earlier checkpoint, or from any checkpoint of another thread, is a fork into a new thread — per the [[DAG]] invariant, "forks and transfers are human-driven only" — so the Checkpoint Graph is where you choose the coherent state, and the [[Composer]] is where you act from it.

A human query has a different primary act: **edit and branch** copies its text and attachments into the composer and forks a new thread from the checkpoint before the original query, sending the edited query as its first message; the original thread is untouched. (Dropped with the Commits reading, 2026-08: **continue exactly here** on an intermediate commit, and the composer prose that named its inclusion boundary. Every continuation point is now a checkpoint's terminal state.)

_(An amendment of 2026-09-04 that made effects follow plan and spec document paths retired with those documents, 2026-09-16. What it kept stands: effects derive from the record, and no generated summary or extra agent reporting is ever needed to show them.)_

## What exists today

Built, in the thread's [[Thread Tabs]] behind the corner icons — under the **Checkpoint Graph** label; the **Checkpoints** label is not there yet — with Thread, Columns, and Graph plus the persisted view choice. Thread walks the checked-out path with its divergence badges; Columns holds branch choices open; Graph lays the whole DAG out as a pannable, zoomable map; picking a commit moves the thread space (navigate, as resolved below); and every view distinguishes published from private work.

**Parked (2026-08): the surface shows Graph alone.** Thread and Columns are withheld for now — their built rows predate the checkpoint reading and still show raw commits, and offering them beside the finished map would present a superseded design as current. This is a shipping posture, not a design change: the three views above, Thread as the default walking reading included, remain the design, and the parked views return rebuilt on continuable checkpoints. While parked, a remembered Thread or Columns choice falls back to Graph — not erased, the same fallback a remembered Columns choice already makes on a linear history — and takes effect again when the views come back. Development builds can re-enable both views from the Experiments page in [[Settings]]; production builds carry no trace of the choice.

Not there yet: the Checkpoints label, the memory and code effects and the glyph that follows them, continuable checkpoints (in flight), edit-and-branch, author-distinct message treatment, and the node popover. Built and now retired by the ruling on [[Mercurian]]: plan and spec effects, and the artifact pane the graph sat beside. The graph reading described above — checkpoint-aware status dots, lineage emphasis, the marked current path, the calm camera, the chosen arrangement and its settings, the minimap — is designed with parts already built. Today's views still expose raw commits uniformly and still place text beside graph nodes. The adopted orchestrator's forks and transfers (2026-09-24) are not drawn at all yet: the built views show one thread and know nothing of its family.

## Open Decisions

### Does picking a commit navigate or preview?

When you select a commit in the Checkpoint Graph, does the thread space immediately move there (checkout semantics), or do you get a peek at that point in the history while staying where you are? Navigate is one fewer concept and makes the Checkpoint Graph the way you move; preview avoids yanking you out of an in-progress thought because you clicked to look. **Recommendation:** navigate — selection moves the thread space, and the Checkpoint Graph highlights your current position. A hover-preview can be layered on later if looking-without-moving proves necessary; a modal "preview mode" probably never should be.

**Resolved (2026-07): navigate, as recommended.** Selection moves the thread space; the Checkpoint Graph highlights where you stand. Hover-preview may layer on later; a modal preview mode shouldn't. _(2026-09-24: a checkpoint in another thread navigates to that thread at that checkpoint; the view choice and camera come along, since they are facts about you.)_

### Do checkpoint kinds get colors?

The map can label nodes or place kind glyphs inside them, but either treatment turns a spatial overview into a field of tiny annotations and disappears unevenly with distance. Color lets the mixture of conversation and standalone acts read while preserving the graph as a clean map. Against it: the palette has to remain meaningful across themes, and it becomes a second visual axis beside the solid/hollow published distinction.

**Resolved (2026-08-19): kinds keep their glyphs; color belongs to status, as corner dots.** Kind never gets a color: the glyph already says what a node is, and it stays — zoom-fading to a plain dot, exactly as built. What gets color is **status**: readiness and staleness, each a small filled dot at the node's top-right corner, replacing the text badges that once floated beside nodes. Text is what leaves the map — names, messages, the current-position caption, and badge text all live in the node popover now. Given up: reading a node's name or message without summoning detail. _(2026-09-16: readiness and staleness both retired with the plan-first ruling; the decision that color means status, in the corner, stands.)_ _(Corrected same day: this resolution was first recorded as "colored status dots, with no glyphs or text on the map" — kind color on the disc, glyphs dropped. That over-read the request, which was to remove the text beside nodes, never the glyphs; and its "status dots" were kind-colored discs, not status at all. The wording above is the real decision.)_

### What does a turn with several effects wear on the map?

_(Raised 2026-09-02, with effects.)_ A turn's disc wears the glyph of what it did, and one turn can amend memory and change code at once. As **one glyph by precedence** — code, then memory — the map answers the question a reader most often brings to it, where code moved, and the popover carries the rest; given up is seeing at a glance that a code turn also touched memory. As a **composite mark** — the disc split into segments, one glyph each — the map is exact, at the cost of legibility: glyphs already fade to plain dots with distance, and a segmented disc fades first, so the exactness holds only up close. As a **"many" glyph** — one symbol meaning more-than-one — the map is honest that there is more and silent about what. **Recommendation:** one glyph by precedence, the popover as the full account. The map exists to show shape, and the standing rule keeps text off it; a single glyph that names the most consequential thing a turn did is the reading that survives zooming out.
