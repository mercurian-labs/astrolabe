# Marketing Site

Mercurian's public face: the page that shows the product to someone who has never opened it. Like [[Visual Language]] and the [[Design Lab]], it sits outside the containment spine — it is a surface about the product, not a place in it. Its governing principle: **the site shows the product itself, not pictures of the product.**

## Show the product, not pictures of it

The site's demonstrations are the product's own surfaces, rendered live on the page from realistic thread histories — not screenshots, not videos, not rebuilt lookalikes. The [[Checkpoint Graph]] carries the weight, because the graph is the brand ([[Design Lab]]): a visitor watches a thread's history grow — a fork opening, branches diverging, code turns landing — inside the real graph surface, and can touch it: switch its views, hover its nodes. (Amended 2026-08: the site deliberately does not demonstrate a **merge** yet — how merging works is being redesigned, and a demonstration of the outgoing behavior would date the moment it lands. Until the redesign ships, every live history on the site is a tree of open branches: forked exploration, no merge commit.) Motion on the site follows the product's own rule ([[Visual Language]]): nothing moves merely to look alive; the scroll story animates only because a thread's state is genuinely changing, and reduced-motion visitors get every beat as stills.

Because the demonstrations are the real surfaces, the site cannot drift from the product: when a component evolves, the site follows in the same change. Keeping the two in sync is not a maintenance task — it is a property of how the site is built.

## The hero

The hero shows the product the way its owner meets it: **open on a desk.** Below a quiet headline, a full-width panel renders a desktop with no operating-system chrome — no menu bar, no dock, nothing but the rendered Mercury globe poster, in its **original neutral stone/taupe coloring** (resolved below), serving as the **wallpaper**. Open on that desk sits the product itself: an **Astrolabe window** — title bar, window controls, and all — whose interior is the real planning space rendered live from a realistic plan history: the planning space's own header — the plan's title, with the two pane toggles in its corner — above the conversation, the real [[Checkpoint Graph]] in its Graph view holding the right pane (the toggles swap it for the plan and spec artifacts, or close it entirely), the [[Composer]] ready beneath. Not a screenshot, not a lookalike — the same components the product ships, so the governing property (the site cannot drift from the product) now extends into the page's most prominent pixels. The mock state is self-referential: the thread on screen is the thread for this very page. The visitor can touch the window's interior at any time — the graph pans and hovers, the composer accepts typing and sends nothing — and picking a checkpoint in the graph rolls the conversation back to that point, the product's own viewing-past posture taking over: the demonstration is the time travel itself, claim 1 happening under the visitor's cursor.

Above the desk: a slim **topbar** — the wordmark (**Fraunces semi-bold, closed by a terracotta full stop**) at its left, and at its right the page's one outward control: a **combined "Try Astrolabe" button carrying the GitHub mark**, which today takes the visitor to the product's public repository and, once a download page exists, will take them there instead (the full resolution of the call-to-action decision, recorded below; the earlier quiet repo link is absorbed into this button) — then modest air (amended 2026-08: the first cut's generous Cursor-scale gap read as too much — the rhythm tightens), then the headline block, which does the naming itself (amended 2026-08: the eyebrow retired — one line of introduction above another read as two openings; amended again the same day): the header is the aphorism **Chart twice, build once.**, and the paragraph beneath opens with the naming-and-positioning sentence — _Astrolabe is your agent development environment for building ambitious software._ — before the branching claim (all pinned below as claim 1, revised). The headline and the close are where the page speaks the product's name (the window's title bar echoes it); the positioning close below the fold names **Astrolabe** as well, so the page opens and closes on the name. The type stays deliberately quiet: the open product is the spectacle, not the words. The page around the desk goes clean and calm in a fixed warm off-white (`#F7F7F3`) — the site wears the light appearance as its own, and the globe's brand moment, exactly where [[Visual Language]] says celestial expression belongs, now lives _inside_ the desk as its wallpaper rather than behind the page, so no text ever needs contrast treatment against it. The window's interior shows as much of the real application as can be rendered honestly: the thread sidebar beside the thread space when the real component can mount on realistic data — never a rebuilt lookalike; a surface that cannot yet mount real simply stays out. The **provider band** (amended 2026-08: it leaves the hero — the desk is the hero's last element, and the band carries claim 3 into the below-fold sections instead; until those sections exist the page runs desk → footer). Nothing in the hero moves on its own; all motion is the visitor's — the autoplay exception recorded for the carousel retires with it, and the motion rule stands unqualified again (amended 2026-08: one successor exception is now designed — the demonstration loop below — recorded, like the carousel's, as deliberate and singular). The window **never takes the page's scroll** (amended 2026-08, replacing the click-to-engage gate: the desk can stand taller than a short viewport, and a window that captures the wheel strands the visitor there). The interior is simply live to the pointer at all times — hover, drag-pan, checkpoint picks, typing — with no engagement step; the conversation pane scrolls natively and hand the wheel back to the page at their ends, as any embedded list does; and the graph's wheel gestures (its pan and zoom) stay with the page — a product affordance the demonstration knowingly forgoes, since the graph still pans by drag and arrives pre-fitted. Given up: wheel-driven graph navigation on the page, and the gate's staging of intent. Below the width where the conversation and the right pane can stand together — a threshold set where they genuinely stop fitting, not a breakpoint above it (amended 2026-08: the first cut collapsed too early) — the window shows the **right pane alone**: the Checkpoint Graph by default, the conversation reachable by closing the pane — because the pane's portrait proportions are a phone's, and the [[#The demonstration loop|demonstration loop]] will inherit the same narrow stage unchanged. When the pane is the stage, its own title bar alone crowns the window, the pane toggles holding the top-right corner exactly as they do beside the conversation; the thread-title header withdraws (two stacked headers read wrong) and returns with the conversation when the pane closes. The positioning statement lives in the page's close, not the hero.

This knowingly breaks the no-drift property for exactly one surface for exactly that long: the hero shows the planning space as it was when it retired, not the thread space that replaced it. Given up, temporarily: the property that the site cannot drift from the product, for the hero alone; recovered when M-187 lands, which also deletes the copy.

**Amended (2026-09-16): the frozen copy follows the ruling.** The copy still renders the retired planning space — plan and spec artifacts behind pane toggles, a self-referential plan — and after the plan-first ruling on [[Mercurian]] it shows a design the product has disowned. ### The demonstration loop

Designed 2026-08 (not yet built; issued separately from the hero's first version): the hero window learns to **demonstrate itself** — a scripted tour, in the desk-hero tradition of self-driving product demos, that walks the open window through the product's story while the visitor watches. The tour is a sequence of **phases**, each showing one pillar on the real surfaces:

1. **Research.** The window opens quiet — no right pane, just the conversation. The graph pane then opens itself, checkpoint popovers surface over the graph's nodes, and the composer _writes and sends messages of its own_: each scripted send lands a new checkpoint in the graph, the history visibly growing the way a real session grows it.
2. **Memory.** The copy above the desk changes to speak for the [[Memory]] system; the composer runs a skill command (the memory map), and the memory section of the product's right sidebar shows its elements in turn.
3. **Building.** The tour closes on the building story — code turns and their diffs shown the way the product shows them, the above-desk copy following the phase.

A **segmented progress bar** sits above the wallpaper panel — one segment per phase, the running phase filling. While the sequence plays, the window is **locked to the visitor**: pointer interaction with the interior does nothing and cannot stop or derail the tour (a deliberate reversal of the pattern's usual interrupt-on-touch — the tour is short, and a half-interrupted demo is worse than either a watched one or a live one). The page's scroll is never captured, playing or not. When the full sequence completes, the window unlocks into the hero's standing behavior — the live interior described above — and the segments become controls: clicking one replays the sequence from that phase's start, locking again for the replay. During playback the segments are inert. Under reduced motion the tour never starts on its own; the segments remain, and a visitor's click still plays a phase — motion at the visitor's initiation is what the rule protects.

This is the hero's one exception to the motion rule, successor to the carousel's retired autoplay exception, and it carries the same discipline: the motion is a thread's state genuinely changing on real surfaces, not decoration. The memory and building phases can only demonstrate what has shipped — the loop's phases arrive as their surfaces do, and a phase with nothing real to show stays out rather than showing a mock.

Claim copy is pinned here so the page cannot drift into marketing paraphrase; the strongest phrases are near-quotes of the design's own invariants. The three claims once took turns in a carousel; they now hold fixed places on the page.

**Claim 1 — Branching planning** (the hero's headline block). Header: _Chart twice, build once._ Copy: _Astrolabe is your agent development environment for building ambitious software. Every message, every plan edit, every code change is a checkpoint in a branching history. Return to any point, take a different direction, explore new ideas side by side, and merge the branches back into a single plan._ (Revised a third time 2026-08: "agent development environment" returns as the positioning phrase; the history sentence tightens and adopts the product's own word — **checkpoint** — in place of "commit in one branching, git-style history"; the closing sentence smooths to a single breath. Supersedes the "branching environment … with coding agents" paragraph, which superseded the _Plan in branches_ form. The compacted-context and nothing-is-destroyed clauses stay retired.) **Amended (2026-09-16):** the copy loses "plan edit" and "into a single plan" under the plan-first ruling on [[Mercurian]]; the pinned text becomes _Every message, every memory amendment, every code change is a checkpoint in a branching history. Return to any point, take a different direction, explore new ideas side by side, and merge the branches back together._ Whether the header _Chart twice, build once._ survives a research-first product is the brand brief's call (M-226), not this note's.

**Claim 2 — Memory** (a below-the-fold section, beside the quiet placeholder that stands in until [[Memory]]'s own graph view exists to show). Header: _Memory you can actually read._ Copy: _Your project's design truth lives as atomic, linked markdown notes in a git repository you own. Nothing is captured behind your back — the assistant proposes an amendment, you confirm it, and it lands as a commit attributed to the thread it came from. Mercurian is a lens over your memory, never a silo for it: every note stays editable with any tool and portable out of the product entirely._

**Claim 3 — Provider-agnostic, local-first, no login** (the provider band, below the fold — amended 2026-08: it moved out of the hero). Header: _Your agents. Your machine. No account._ Copy: _Mercurian works with the coding agents you already have — Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and more — switchable between turns, even mid-thread, with every branch recording exactly what it ran under. It runs on your machine, against your repositories, with no sign up required. History and memory are plain text and git: yours to keep, wherever you go next._ (Amended 2026-08: "no signup and no cloud in the loop" became "no sign up required". Amended 2026-09-16: "plans" left the copy under the plan-first ruling. "No account" and the teams-first audience on [[Mercurian]] must be reconciled before this claim is re-pinned.)

A deliberate caveat travels with this copy: claim 2 describes [[Memory]] as designed, not as built — its note records "not built" today — and claim 1's merge clause slightly leads the build (branching and history are shipped; merge commits are not yet — and with merging under redesign, the site's demonstrations show no merge either, per the amendment above: the copy may promise merging, the graphs may not picture it). These are the page's only promises, made knowingly; revisit this section when either lands.

## Copy

The site's claims are the design's own words. Positioning and section copy draw on the vault's canonical invariants — every step is a checkpoint, forks and merges are human-driven, grounded in your connected repositories, memory you can read ([[Memory]]) — quoted, not paraphrased into marketing prose. Copy written this way ages with the design instead of drifting from it, for the same reason the components cannot drift.

## Type

Three voices, each deliberate (ruled 2026-08). **Fraunces** speaks for Mercurian itself — the wordmark and the page's headline, nothing else. **Inter** (self-hosted, like every asset on the page) carries the page's copy — topbar, paragraphs, footer — so the site reads identically on every platform instead of shifting with the visitor's OS. And inside the hero window, the type is **the visitor's own system stack**, because the window renders the real product and the product genuinely speaks the platform's font; giving the window Inter would be the one way the "not a screenshot" claim could quietly lie. The carve-out is scoped exactly like the window's other demo-scale overrides. The **Try Astrolabe** control wears the desk wallpaper's near-black ground with light type (ruled 2026-08-29) — the page's one action is also its strongest element, and the light-rendered GitHub mark rides the text color. A shared link carries the **card**: the prototype's golden-globe social preview (the wordmark over the full-bleed Mercury globe, no tagline — the card cannot drift from copy it does not carry), recovered from the prototype's iMessage-tuned treatment and shipped as a raster at standard card size on an absolute URL so message scrapers actually render it. (Corrected 2026-08-31: an earlier pass ported the constellation og-card.svg, which was never the card that rendered in messages.)

**The demo answers** (ruled 2026-08-30). The hero window is no longer read-only theater: a visitor who types into the composer gets a real exchange on fixture state — their message lands as a checkpoint parented where they stand, the graph grows the way the product grows it (sending from an earlier point opens a branch, with the product's own composer notice saying so first), and the reply — "To try Astrolabe, checkout the project here." — lands as a checkpoint of its own, with a Stop that genuinely cancels; its "here" is a live link to the repository and retargets to the download page when one exists, exactly like the Try Astrolabe button. The composer opens empty behind the suggestion "Research, fork, or build..." (2026-09-16; formerly "Research, plan, or build...") — a placeholder proposes, it never presumes. The window opens mid-turn: a fixture human ask ("Fold the fixture approach into memory.") stands as a pending checkpoint with its reply streaming, and on completion the reply lands _inside that same checkpoint_ — no new node, exactly the product's shape — with the landed copy in the done tense ("The fixture approach is folded into memory."). The opening liveness and the interactivity stop contradicting each other. The model selector is the product's real picker on fixture providers, actually selectable. Two controls leave the composer — Implement and image attach — a scoped carve-out of the "real chrome is the honesty" posture: a control that cannot act in the demo is less honest present than absent. Nothing persists; reload returns the fixture. This send machinery is the substrate [[The demonstration loop]]'s scripted phases will drive.

## What exists today

Nothing Mercurian-branded is live. ## Open Decisions

### Retrofit the inherited marketing app, or start fresh?

**Resolved (2026-08): start fresh, beside the product.** The inherited marketing app is upstream's, still receiving upstream's changes; adopting it would mean inheriting its content and merging against its future. The Mercurian site starts new inside the product's own codebase — next to the components it renders, which is what makes the no-drift property possible — and the inherited app dies at cut-over as already planned. Given up: whatever head start the inherited app's structure offered, which on inspection was none.

### Which globe does the hero wear?

**Resolved (2026-08): the still poster in its original neutral stone/taupe coloring.** The prototype produced two hero treatments — a live animated globe and a high-fidelity still rendered from real Mercury elevation data — and two colorings, neutral stone and a later gold. The hero uses the still, in the original neutral coloring. Given up: the animated globe's motion and its ability to repaint with the page's theme; the hero stays a fixed image, and the site's liveliness lives in the product demonstrations instead.

**Amended (2026-09-16, with the identity direction on [[Visual Language]]): the neutral still stays until the approved terrain's hero export ships, then swaps.** The choice above was made between two prototype renders; the identity ruling since approved one terrain treatment for everything — the olive and brass flat map in its palette colors — and the site is the one surface still wearing something else. The swap is a planned step, like the download-page retarget: the wallpaper changes when the branding release delivers the hero export, not before, and stays a still either way. Given up: the neutral poster as a permanent second terrain palette, which would have fragmented the identity between site and product.

### Does a waitlist survive onto the new site?

**Resolved (2026-08): no.** The prototype's waitlist, and the machinery behind it, do not carry over. The first version of the site informs; it does not collect signups. Given up: capturing interested visitors — which sharpens the next question.

### What does the site ask a visitor to do?

With the waitlist gone, the page has no defined next step. The candidates differ in what they promise: pointing at the product's repository invites source-first visitors but implies the code is the offer; a contact route invites conversation but scales poorly; no call to action at all keeps the page honest while the product isn't yet available, at the cost of collecting nobody. **Recommendation:** while the product has no public access story, let the page close on the positioning rather than a hollow signup — and revisit the moment there is something real to offer, since this decision dates quickly.

**Partially resolved (2026-08): the topbar carries a quiet link to the public repository.** Ruled with the desk hero: the page's one outward pointer is a repository link in the topbar — an offer to source-first visitors, deliberately quiet rather than a button. The close still ends on positioning with no signup and no other call to action; whether the page ever asks for more than "read the source" remains open and still dates quickly. Given up: the strictest no-next-step posture — the page now does imply the code is on offer, knowingly.

**Resolved (2026-08, later): a combined "Try Astrolabe" button.** The quiet link grows into the page's one call to action and absorbs the repository pointer: a topbar button reading **Try Astrolabe**, carrying the GitHub mark, taking the visitor to the public repository — the honest "try" while installing means building from source. **When a download page exists, the button points there instead**; that future destination is recorded here deliberately, so the swap is a planned step rather than a redesign. The close still ends on positioning, now naming the product. Given up: the deliberately-quiet-not-a-button posture — the page now asks for something, and says the product's name while asking.

### Does the hero make one claim or several?

**Resolved (2026-08): several — a three-slide carousel, manually advanced.** The single positioning statement gave the hero one claim and pushed the product's strongest differentiators — branching planning, readable memory, provider-agnostic local-first privacy — below the fold. The hero now cycles three pinned claims beside the globe (copy pinned above), advanced only by the visitor; autoplay stays excluded by the motion rule. The positioning line moves to the page's close. Given up: the single-utterance hero — and with several slides, a claim can go unseen; mitigated by leading with the strongest one.

**Amended (2026-08, later): autoplay permitted, and slides carry visuals.** Ruled deliberately over the earlier reading: the carousel may advance on a timer — the hero is the page's one expressive moment, the motion is the claims taking turns, and it stops the moment the visitor engages (hover or controls) and under reduced-motion preferences. Each slide now pairs copy with a visual (the real Checkpoint Graph, the memory placeholder, the provider marks), and the globe moves behind the carousel as background. Given up: the strictest form of the no-ambient-motion rule on this one surface — recorded here as the exception that proves it, not a precedent for working surfaces.

**Amended again (2026-08, final): the carousel retires — the hero becomes the product on its desk.** Ruled deliberately: instead of claims taking turns beside the globe, the hero renders a chrome-less desktop whose wallpaper is the globe poster, with an Astrolabe window open on it — the real planning space, live, in self-referential mock state (the desk-hero pattern of desktop-product sites, done one better: our window's interior is the product's real components, not a picture). The three claims keep their pinned copy but stop rotating: claim 1 becomes the hero's headline block, claim 3 becomes the provider band under the desk, claim 2 becomes a below-the-fold section. Given up: the carousel and everything ruled for it — the autoplay exception retires unexercised-by-anything-else, restoring the motion rule to its unqualified form; the timer-as-indicator segments go with it; and the globe stops being the page's background (with the words-over-globe contrast treatment made unnecessary), becoming the desk's wallpaper instead.

### Does the hero window invite play?

The hero's Astrolabe window could behave like a real window — draggable across the desk, resizable from its edges — proving liveness through manipulation the way desktop-product sites sometimes do. Or it can sit fixed while only its _interior_ is live — the graph pans, the composer types — which already proves the components are real. The cost of window play is real: drag and resize machinery is page furniture with no product analogue, and a resizable window puts the product's responsive behavior on public display at every width, a surface that must then be kept presentable. **Recommendation:** the interior's liveness ships first and is the point; window manipulation is deliberate polish to revisit once the desk hero has earned its place — not part of its first version.

### Do visitors get to repaint the page?

The product's demonstrations could wear both appearances — a visitor flipping the page between light and dark (or between themes) would see the demos repaint live, proving the components are real and marketing the theming system in the same gesture. The cost is a control that must not upstage the content, and a second appearance to keep presentable forever. **Recommendation:** yes, as a single quiet appearance flip scoped to proving liveness — full theme-picking stays in the product.

### When does the demonstration loop start, and does it repeat?

The demonstration loop's trigger and afterlife are undecided. Starting on page load spends the tour while the visitor is still reading the headline; starting when the desk scrolls into view plays to an audience but adds an observer and a dormant window state. After completing, the tour could rest (the window becomes the live interior and stays so) or begin again unattended (truer to the pattern's origin, but ambient motion without end sits worst against the motion rule). **Recommendation:** start when the desk first enters view, run once, rest; the segments make replay a one-click act, which is enough afterlife for a demo.
