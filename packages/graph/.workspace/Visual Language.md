# Visual Language

Astrolabe's visual language is the product-wide expression of how Mercurian works: a calm, precise instrument for research. It makes the conversation feel like the workshop, the [[Checkpoint Graph]] feel like a map of the thinking so far, and the project's [[Memory|memory]] feel like what the work is for. _(2026-09-16: reframed with the plan-first ruling on [[Mercurian]]; the plan artifact this note once centred is gone.)_ It applies across surfaces rather than living in any one place in the product, so it sits outside the containment spine.

## Hierarchy follows the product

The interface should explain Mercurian's model before its labels do. Conversation is active and chronological. The Checkpoint Graph is spatial and structural: it reveals position, ancestry, forks, and merges without turning the whole thread space into a diagram. Memory is the standing surface — stable, authored, the thing the conversation returns to.

The [[Left Sidebar]] stays dense and calm. Status is economical — one strongest signal per thread — and project context remains secondary to the work itself. The [[Composer]] is the human control surface: suggestions are offers, not actions, and its appearance should preserve the invariant that the user opens forks and chooses when the assistant acts.

## A restrained instrument

Astrolabe should feel purpose-built without becoming theatrical. Instrument-like details belong where they carry meaning: calibrated spacing, fine rules, a consistent node-and-line grammar, precise selection and focus treatments, and a small family of glyphs for events in history. Ordinary controls remain ordinary enough to recognize immediately.

One rule of the node-and-line grammar holds product-wide: **a dashed line means compacted context.** Wherever history reaches the present as summary rather than verbatim, the line carrying it draws dashed; solid lines stay reserved for verbatim continuity, so the stroke itself answers whether something arrived whole. Its first instance is the [[Merges|merge]]: the [[Checkpoint Graph]]'s map keeps the taking thread's line solid and draws a dashed edge from the checkpoint whose context was taken in as compaction. Any future surface that draws a line across a compaction boundary inherits the same stroke.

The astrolabe metaphor is structural, not illustrative. Celestial ornament, glow, gradients, and ambient motion do not spread across the shell merely to repeat the name. Launch and initial loading can carry more expression; the working surfaces, including empty states, stay quiet.

## Color, type, and elevation

Color communicates state and action before it communicates brand. The product's [[Status Vocabulary]] — attention, lifecycle, gates, interruption, selection, and focus — remains distinguishable without relying on hue alone. A restrained accent gives Astrolabe identity and marks intentional action; it does not tint every surface. Light and dark appearances preserve the same hierarchy rather than becoming separate designs.

Typography separates roles without multiplying voices: compact, highly legible chrome around a more deliberate reading surface for notes and files. Code, repository facts, and fixed-width identifiers may use a monospaced voice; product prose should not inherit it by default. Elevation is similarly spare — mostly flat relationships, with raised surfaces reserved for things that temporarily sit above the work, such as palettes, sheets, and popovers.

## Motion

Nothing moves merely to look alive. Motion explains a change in position, hierarchy, or state: opening a branch, moving through history, revealing a pane, or showing where an action landed. Working indicators remain cheap and quiet, reduced-motion preferences preserve every meaning, and no continuously repainting treatment is part of the visual identity.

## Themes and customization

Astrolabe's identity lives in hierarchy, geometry, typography, and interaction as much as in one palette. Theme variations may change colors while preserving semantic contrast, status priority, graph legibility, and focus visibility. This principle does not itself decide which appearance controls Mercurian ships; that reconciliation remains with [[Settings]]. The [[Design Lab]] is the instrument for exploring these choices on the running product, and its guardrails are this note's rules made checkable.

## The chosen direction

**Ruled (2026-09-16): the instrument.** Two directions were compared on the same thread screen in both appearances — a flagship warm-stone pair with the instrument concentrated where it carries meaning, against the adopted shell's appearance untouched with identity carried by wordmark, symbol, and one accent. The second was a real alternative, not a palette swap: it also gave up the signature composer controls, the graph's pivot, and terrain inside the product. The instrument won because the branching, checkpointed way of working — research, fork, parallelize, review — is the product's distinction, and a shell that looks like every other agent client hides it; what the quieter direction saved was mostly work the theme roles absorb anyway. Given up: the cheapest possible refresh, and the freedom to leave the composer and graph exactly as inherited.

A one-page brand brief lives beside the identity sources in the branding repository — audience, promise, personality, and naming — and every downstream asset is judged against it. Its naming rule belongs here too: **Mercurian** is the company, with the wordmark Mercurian closed by a rust full stop; **Astrolabe** is the product — app name, titles, icon, sidebar, links, calls to action; together, _Astrolabe by Mercurian_, never a compound. Claims stay inside the first milestone's workflow: what is designed but not shipped is described as designed.

**Where identity lives.** The flagship pair ships as the product's default appearance, expressed entirely through the semantic roles so inherited themes, the theme editor, and imports keep working. In light, the **working surface is alabaster** — the thread and its artifacts sit on a near-white ground for the highest reading contrast (ruled 2026-09-16), and the warmer ivory and sand tones are kept to the chrome around it: sidebar, panel strips, the composer's frame. Dark is charcoal with ivory text; whether its working surface deepens toward near-black for the same contrast is deliberately left to the flagship appearance work, judged on real threads in the [[Design Lab]] rather than on a sheet. Rust is the one accent in both. The instrument shows in exactly four places: the **symbol and the wordmark that carries it** — the wordmark headed the [[Left Sidebar]] from 2026-09-28 to 2026-09-30 and was removed for the space it took, so it appears on no working surface _(2026-09-28, Venkat; the guideline in the branding repository still says 18 px and stands for every other surface)_ — (a monogram: the wordmark typeface's capital A with the alidade needle laid along its stem and the rust pivot, ringed by negative space, cut through the letter so that the ring severs the letter's foot — the severing is the mark's signature; ruled 2026-09-17 over the ticked dial that was approved earlier the same day, because the letter ties the symbol to the wordmark's typeface and carries the instrument as structure alone); the **[[Composer]]'s four controls** — send, stop, queue, steer — sharing one dot-and-ring pivot whose ring is transparent, reserved for those four actions while ordinary actions keep the standard utility icons _(amended 2026-09-27, Venkat: the controls are duotone, and the pivot dot is the icon's own second tone — the stroke colour at about half strength — rather than the accent; "the red dot doesn't have to be red". In the product they sit bare, without a disc, at 28px. The masters in the branding repository keep the rust pivot for the marks; the product's reading is this one)_; the **[[Checkpoint Graph]]**, where the node you stand on is the pivot and every other node stays plain (whether the motif becomes the general node glyph is the graph's own decision, still open); and the **brand moments** — launch and initial loading, before the main app opens — which alone may carry the planet's terrain (the flat map in the olive and stone alternating-contrast palette — sixteen bands, dark olive and light stone interleaved so neighbouring elevations contrast — in its pure palette colors; confirmed 2026-09-17 over the gradual olive and stone reversal, the alabaster and mineral palettes, the globe crops, and the gradient variant). Everywhere else the product stays ordinary: inherited components, standard icons, quiet working surfaces. The grammar that survives a palette change — fine rules, tight corners, the node-and-line rules, the pivot geometry — is what makes the product recognizable in a theme it did not choose.

**Terrain on brand moments, and their motion.** Terrain is shown **zoomed in** — a crop of the map, never the whole of it — so it reads as calm ground rather than busy texture, and wherever text or the lockup sits on it, a **slightly translucent wash** of the surface color lies between terrain and text so the words stay legible; the wash is part of the treatment (ruled 2026-09-16). The motion rule above stands unqualified: nothing moves merely to look alive. Exactly one surface may carry moving terrain — the **loading moment**, where the zoomed crop makes one slow, eighteen-second pan to show that loading is in progress, and stops earlier if the product becomes usable (duration confirmed 2026-09-19). Launch carries the terrain as a still. Pairing and empty states do not carry terrain. Every moving brand moment has a static reduced-motion equivalent that carries the same meaning: the same crop under the same wash, without the drift. The terrain never loops, and the [[Marketing Site]]'s wallpaper stays a still.

**Launch and initial-loading splash confirmed (2026-09-19).** The current visual placement study is the approved design to build for launch and initial loading. This confirms the splash design, not a shipped implementation. The eighteen-second pan is confirmed. The loading panel shows the full Astrolabe wordmark alone, without a separate monogram, subtitle, or visible status copy; its padding tightens around the wordmark while preserving clear space, and its corner radius and shadow match the inherited floating panel. Loading remains identifiable to assistive technology. The wordmark's rust full stop is the first of three matching dots: keep the first visible, progressively reveal the second and third, then reset, with space reserved so the wordmark never shifts. The confirmed loading dots use a 1.6-second rhythm (one dot for 400 ms, two for 400 ms, three for 800 ms, with 120 ms opacity transitions). This small repeating progress indication is an exception to the no-loop rule, not permission for looping terrain. Reduced motion shows all three dots and the terrain completely still.

**Scope amendment (2026-09-19): launch and loading only.** The new terrain and wordmark treatment is confined to launch and initial loading before the main app opens. Pairing, including its pending, success, and failure states, keeps the inherited component. Startup recovery, WSL connection and fallback, native startup errors, and terminal authorization also retain their existing treatments; being before the main app does not itself qualify a surface for terrain. Once the main app has loaded, terrain never appears in its content: first-run setup, an empty thread list, active work, reconnecting, and subsequent waits all use the existing components. This supersedes the earlier allowance for pairing and empty-state terrain. The choice gives up extra branded moments to keep the app close to upstream and reduce merge-conflict overhead. It does not reverse the separate development app-icon or marketing-site decisions.

**Conflicts named and resolved.** The [[Marketing Site]]'s hero wears the neutral stone/taupe still poster by an earlier ruling made between two prototype renders, before the olive and brass flat map existed; the site keeps that poster until the approved terrain's hero export ships through a branding release, then swaps as a planned step — one terrain treatment everywhere, eventually, rather than two palettes forever (recorded under that decision on [[Marketing Site]]). The site's own type ruling — the display face for wordmark and headline, a self-hosted text face for copy — is the page's, not the product's; the product uses the display face for the wordmark alone until its typography is decided. The refresh-scope assessment's reading that the identity is "a thin brand layer, not a component redesign" holds: the direction touches theme roles, four icons, one graph node treatment, and the moments before work — nothing else. The symbol's pivot and the controls' pivot were briefly two proportions of the same motif; the symbol now takes the controls' dot-and-ring ratio exactly (2026-09-17), so the motif is one proportion everywhere it appears. Because the symbol is the wordmark typeface's own A, the Astrolabe wordmark's initial and the symbol are the same glyph; the lockup work started from that rather than placing the mark beside a second A. **Ruled (2026-09-17): the Astrolabe wordmark is its own horizontal lockup** — the monogram as the initial, the rest of the name in the display face, closed by the rust full stop — so the symbol is never set beside it; the original stacked-lockup allowance for launch, pairing, empty states, and square crops was superseded for these app surfaces on 2026-09-19: launch/loading uses the wordmark alone and the remaining flows retain their inherited components; and the symbol alone serves spaces too small for either. **Mercurian** keeps its capitalized wordmark and has no lockup, because it has no symbol; a Mercurian monogram may be drawn later, and until then a surface that names the company shows the wordmark or plain text. The marks' sizes, clear space, and colorways are asset facts kept with the masters in the branding repository, not product intent; what the product must honour is that a mark never sits on terrain without the wash, and that below a mark's minimum size the name is set as text rather than a shrunken mark. **The app icon's channels (ruled 2026-09-17):** the icon's ground tells the release channel apart — alabaster for production, charcoal for nightly, and a still, zoomed crop of the terrain for the development build — and the ground belongs to the channel, so no icon carries a light or dark appearance variant; an icon that does not match the desktop's appearance is ordinary, and the icon is judged by reading correctly on both. The development icon is the one place a mark sits on terrain without the wash and outside the brand moments: a deliberate exception for a build only maintainers install, not a precedent for any shipped surface. Mobile keeps the adapted shell's identity for now, as the [[Mobile App]] decided: rebranding it is a launch decision, and its deferral is explicit rather than accidental.

## What exists today

Astrolabe carries the Mercurian name and identity while most of its component language and appearance controls still come from the shell it adapts — including a full appearance system users can already customize: several built-in themes, an in-app theme editor, and importing themes from a code editor. The first Mercurian-specific grammar is emerging in the thread space and Checkpoint Graph, but the product-wide visual language has not yet been applied consistently. A light-direction exploration — warm flat surfaces, a distinctive chrome typeface, functional color, and a DAG shape grammar — was sketched but the sketches themselves are lost; only this recorded description survives, so the direction is a remembered starting point, not shipped design and not a recoverable artifact. The [[Design Lab]]'s profiles are designed to prevent exactly this loss: a direction rebuilt there is saved, switchable, and proposable. A read-only design-system catalog also exists — foundations, component states, and an audit — at its own address with its own chrome; the [[Design Lab]] absorbs and redesigns it.

## Open Decisions

### How strongly should the instrument metaphor shape the interface?

At one pole, a recognizable visual motif reaches across chrome, artifacts, navigation, and history; Astrolabe looks unmistakable in any crop, but the metaphor can become costume. At the other, the product remains almost entirely utilitarian and relies on wordmark and color for identity; it stays familiar, but gives up the opportunity for the history model itself to feel distinctive. **Recommendation:** keep the metaphor structural and concentrate it in the Checkpoint Graph and memory. Let geometry, type, and state grammar create recognition across the rest of the product, with expressive illustration reserved for rare brand moments.

**Resolved (2026-09-16): structural and concentrated, as recommended.** The metaphor lives in the symbol, the composer's four controls, the graph's pivot, and the brand moments, and nowhere else; see [[#The chosen direction]]. The alternative was judged on the same thread screen and rejected because it left the way of working looking like any other agent client. Given up: an unmistakable-in-any-crop motif across chrome and navigation — the working surfaces stay quiet by design, so a crop of the sidebar or the timeline alone reads as a calm tool, not as Astrolabe.

### Is Astrolabe defined by one flagship palette or by a theme-safe grammar?

A single flagship palette creates the strongest immediate identity and the smallest design target. A theme-safe grammar lets people adapt a tool they inhabit all day, but identity must survive colors the product did not choose. This fork is not hypothetical: the shell Astrolabe adapts already ships user customization — built-in themes, a theme editor, imported editor themes — so the flagship-only pole means taking away something people can do today, and the rework must decide the fate of those inherited themes either way. **Recommendation:** design and ship a flagship light and dark pair, while defining every component through semantic roles so the customization users already have can survive if [[Settings]] retains it. Astrolabe should remain recognizable from its hierarchy and geometry when the palette changes.

**Resolved (2026-09-16): both, as recommended — a flagship pair shipped through the theme-safe grammar.** The warm-stone light and dark pair is the product's default appearance and its strongest identity; it is expressed only through the semantic roles, so the customization people already have — built-in themes, the theme editor, imported themes — survives untouched, and the instrument's geometry, controls, and graph grammar carry recognition under any palette. The [[Settings]] reconciliation keeps the inherited theming; this ruling takes nothing away. Given up: the smaller design target of a flagship-only product, and the option of a palette that no theme could ever override.
